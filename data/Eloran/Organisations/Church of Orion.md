# Church of Orion

> Part of: [[Eloran]] | See also: [[People/Orion]], [[Organisations/Order of Maegi]], [[Cosmology]]

The dominant religion of the Eloran continent. Worships Orion as Saint and Martyr — the mortal who saved the world from Erathon's corruption and sealed the Veil to protect humanity from itself. The Church teaches that magic in any form is a curse, that the Art killed its own master, and that the suppression of Veil-wielders is a sacred duty.

The Church is a lie built by guilty people, that grew beyond them, and is now hunting their descendants.

The faithful call the centuries since Orion's sacrifice the **Age of Salvation** (_Věk spásy_) — the long peace won by the Saint's seal, however dark the world grows.

---

## Public Doctrine

- Orion was a mortal of unmatched virtue who confronted the great demon Erathon
- He sacrificed himself to imprison Erathon and seal the world from corruption
- The Veil — magic — is a corrupting force; those who wield it are eventually consumed
- The Templars are the protective hand of the Saint, charged with destroying Veil-wielders
- The world's slow withering is the price humanity must pay for safety from Erathon
- Tyr and the Forgotten Realms pantheon are recognized as lesser celestial powers, but the Saint's cult is supreme on Eloran

## Hidden Truth

Founded by the nine disciples who betrayed Orion during the sealing ritual. Designed as a cover-up: a heroic narrative wrapped around their crime to ensure no one looked too closely at what really happened beneath Ellesmere.

It worked. It worked too well. The Church became its own institution and outlasted its founders. The militant arm — the Templars — eventually took over the doctrine and turned anti-Veil enforcement into a continental project. The Maegi lineages, including the descendants of the disciples themselves, became targets.

---

## Structure

### The Templars (the Just Ones)

The militant arm. Originally founded by Ilrusk and others among the nine disciples. Has grown into the dominant power within the Church. Hunts and destroys Veil-wielders — fire and sword, no trial.

Symbol: golden plate gauntlet on a white field. Senior templars have stars above the gauntlet marking rank.

### The Hammer of Orion

Elite militant faction within the Templars. Symbol: gauntlet crossed with a warhammer. Tasked with the most dangerous magical targets — including pursuing Erathon fragments and the survivors of Ellesmere.

### Local Faction in Orestor

[[Places/Orestor]] hosts a Templar branch — the surviving paladin order from Meredie's destruction. Known as **Orionův hněv** (Orion's Wrath) in Meredic. Named templars: **Jaskell** (huge blonde, masterwork two-handed sword), **Jimmy** (short, stocky, bald with black beard; shield, axe, mace). Arci-Templar: **Lucian Tillibor**.

The **Just Ones** in Hlubina (paladin order from Meredie's destruction) is part of the same institution.

---

## Severite / Mlčoun

The Church's most distinctive tool: a material that suppresses the Veil. Used in shackles, blades, manacles, and reinforced armor for hunting Veil-wielders.

- **Severite** — Common/Imperial term (also: _oddělovač_ — "separator")
- **Mlčoun** — Meredic term (from _mlčet_, to be silent — "the silencer")

Both names refer to the same material. Cultural use varies by region; Imperial Templars favor _Severite_, Meredic Templars favor _Mlčoun_.

**Hidden truth:** Severite was created by Ilrusk himself, who provided the knowledge to forge it when the Templars were founded. Why he gave the institution that would eventually hunt his own kind the means to do so is one of the great open questions of the Veiling.

## Tensions

- **Orion's Heralds** ([[Places/Orestor]]) — magic-positive faction wanting to restore the Art. Considered heretics by the Church but operate openly in Orestor due to weak central control there
- **Order of Maegi** survivors — descendants of the disciples; hunted as the most dangerous heretics; many in hiding
- **Erathon fragments** — the Hammer of Orion pursues these aggressively; the Church does not publicly acknowledge that the saint's bones may be inside them
