# Orestor — Selith: motives and nature

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]]
> **Discussion draft; not promoted to canon.** The motives and nature of [[People/Selith]] remain under development.
> Related preparation: [the bank commission](orestor-fragment-commission.md), [the timeline](orestor-sacred-timeline.md), [the independent Herald rites](orestor-herald-rituals.md).

## Motivation

Selith combines **the faithful creation** and **the discarded predecessor**. Orion created him before the nine disciples and the completed structure of the Art. Selith remained devoted to his creator. He resents the disciples for receiving Orion's attention, power, and legacy, then betraying him.

His proposed ultimate goal is to **gather all the fragments**. He understands absorbing Orion's essence as resurrecting Orion through himself and continuing his work. He believes he is becoming Orion returned. The historical Orion's intentions need not match Selith's interpretation of them.

**Developing direction:** Selith cannot contain all that power himself. He intends to distribute what he considers Orion's essence among his followers, promising to make them demigods. He can understand Orion's return as occurring through himself and the faithful joined to him. Actual divinity, successful purification, and the amount he can personally retain remain unresolved.

In his mind, devotion, reunion with his creator, growing power, and vindication serve the same purpose. By gathering Orion and completing his work, he proves that he was the faithful creation worthy of carrying the legacy.

Possible expression: “They were given his power and betrayed him. I will gather what they left of him, and finish his work.”

Learning of the fragment in Orestor gives him an opportunity within that larger ambition. His campaign of salvation and punishment has its own motive and can operate without the stone. The original betrayers are beyond Selith's reach. Ilrusk survives in Orestor and is currently stronger, but Selith does not know he is there.

## Waiting and awakening

Working history: Orion left Selith waiting thousands of years ago. Whether he directly imposed dormancy or instructed Selith to await his return remains open. Selith lay dormant somewhere in [[Places/Ellesmere|Ellesmere]] until the **Shattering / Roztříštění at PS 0** released and awakened him. The precise location, means of confinement, and Orion's intentions need deciding.

Awakening resembles recovery from a long coma. Selith has partial awareness of events but lacks details and context. His knowledge needs sources; dormancy does not itself give him continuous knowledge of history. Impressions during dormancy and information acquired after awakening are possible sources.

Selith may have understood waiting as an act of fidelity. He wakes into a world where Orion is absent, the disciples are celebrated, and institutions speak in his name. What he remembers as a promise of return need not explain Orion's actual reason for leaving him.

Awakening in Ellesmere and learning of the fragment's arrival in Orestor are separate events. Orestor draws him as Orion's city. His journey, discovery of the changed world, and cultivation of the Zvěstovatelé during PS 0–PS 2 remain to be developed.

## Before the ship arrives — early followers to develop

Existing [[People/Selith]] and [[Organisations/Orionovi Zvěstovatelé]] notes place Selith in Orestor, visiting and marking people before the opening. His followers call him **Orion**. Establish the first host or conduit and a small named cast: public lives, motives, progressive corruption, quirks, abilities, limits, recruitment, and Day 1 whereabouts. At least one should be reachable through ordinary city life. This starting cast and visible activity remain open within the Day 1 working scope and can be developed through later amendments. The full ritual sequence and later arc remain follow-ups, and the activity is independent of Bery's fragment. See [the discussion queue](orestor-preparation-questions.md).

## Selith and Orion's churches

Selith appears as Orion and offers salvation he believes is real. His anger concerns institutions built on the founding lie:

- **[[Organisations/Apostolic Church of Orion|Apoštolská církev]]** preserves the account that conceals the disciples' betrayal.
- **[[Organisations/Order of the Just|Spravedliví]]** justify suppressing magic through Orion, although Orion himself created and structured the Art.
- **[[Organisations/Orion's Wrath|Orionův hněv]]**, in Selith's view, continues this appropriation of Orion's legacy. Tillibor's demand that Hněv monopolize control of magic sharpens this conflict with Orion's legacy.

“False prophets” refers to these institutions' claim to speak for Orion; it does not establish another group of prophets. Selith regards their activity as a continuation of the betrayal. This does not establish how much ordinary believers or individual leaders actually know.

## Nature and followers

Working proposal: Selith is an emotionally volatile being made more of Veil energy than of a permanent body. He exists outside the Art's structure. His capabilities can be more varied than Maegi methods, while his current strength is substantially below Ilrusk's.

Breadth of ability, strength, precision, and reliability are separate questions. Existing outside the Art does not itself establish immunity to Maegi, Mlčoun, or physical harm to a host.

**Imbuing a person with Veil:** Selith gradually gains greater influence over their will. Something resembling a connected corruption develops among his followers. Resistance is possible at least up to a point; the threshold remains open. Whether entry requires an invitation, how full possession works, the number of simultaneous hosts, range, and access to memories need deciding.

A host's death does not substantially threaten Selith physically in the working direction, though exact consequences remain open. He may nevertheless care deeply about his followers. Losing someone who believed in him means something different to him from losing a usable body. Affection and progressively overriding a follower's will can coexist in his thinking.

**Veil absorption is conditional.** The mere presence of life, magic, or a fragment is insufficient. Prerequisites, prior experience, and the cost remain open. Selith recognizes corruption in Orion's essence; how precisely he understands Erathon's share is undecided.

If a rite involves sacrificing a follower, that choice creates a further tension with this affection. His justification, the follower's understanding and ability to refuse, and whether he mourns the loss need development. A possible belief is that the sacrifice participates in the faithful's shared elevation; that belief is not proof the donor survives within them.

## Resurrection as a conviction

| Layer                      | Working description                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Belief of the Zvěstovatelé | Orion has returned to save the faithful and cleanse sinners.                                                                |
| Selith's conviction        | Absorbing Orion's essence resurrects him through Selith and continues his work. Sharing it elevates the faithful.           |
| Immediate aim              | Establish his promised salvation and punish perceived oath-breakers; pursue the fragment when he learns of it.              |
| Actual outcome             | Open: changes to identity, will, and power; what remains of Selith, Orion, and Erathon; what the recipients really receive. |

Whether Selith already considers resurrection underway, and when he would call it complete, remain open. His certainty establishes neither Orion's approval nor the actual separability of the essences.

## Ilrusk: knowledge of a record, not the being

Ilrusk knows from Orion's notes that **something like this was created**. His own assessment marked it as a dead end: excessively devoted and unstable, relating to its creator more like a dog or child than an independent person. This is Ilrusk's judgment from records, not personal acquaintance with Selith or an impartial measure of his intelligence.

**Ilrusk does not know the being escaped Ellesmere. Selith has no information about Ilrusk's survival and current identity.** The clues that could lead to recognition need preparation. Ilrusk can investigate the cult and attacks without immediately connecting them to an ancient entry.

The DM's proposal for Ilrusk: he is well past his prime. Experiments with life energy and transferring his mind, following his takeover of his apprentice's body, cost him dearly. The Mlčenliví serve as instruments of control and protection. Declining abilities do not by themselves determine the durability of the body he has spent centuries improving in canon; the specific costs remain open.

At the top of **Nebeská čtvrť**, **Orionův starý dům** may stand behind stone doors accessible only to Ilrusk. There he has spent the last millennium studying records and reproducing Orion's experiments. The house, means of access, and surviving records are proposals. They do not establish personal acquaintance with every creation or knowledge of its current manifestations.

Reliable evidence can change Ilrusk's approach. His ignorance should not persist regardless of evidence and player actions. His response begins with available people and knowledge, rather than automatically producing a personal confrontation.

## Ritual and victims — scope of further discussion

The [Herald rites](orestor-herald-rituals.md) assert the cult's presence and punish perceived betrayals. They begin and continue independently of the fragment's location or Selith's possession of it. Their magic, costs, and timing remain open. Blood or a follower's sacrifice is a possibility, not an established requirement.

Selith's desire to share Orion's essence with the faithful remains a separate ambition. How he might use a recovered fragment, and whether separation is possible, need fresh discussion. The [separation and distribution mechanism](orestor-fragment-ritual-archived.md) is parked and does not explain the independent rites.

Selith may select people who, in his judgment, betrayed a person, faith, oath, or duty. Individual cases will be discussed gradually. Candidates are not established crimes or a fixed roster. His accusations must remain distinct from the actual circumstances.

**Tillibor** is a possible later victim, with no fixed place in a sequence. His character and the origins and abilities of templars need discussion first. The older [Spravedliví proposals](order-of-the-just-wip.md) are material for discussion, not accepted answers.

The working plan is **nine intended deaths echoing Orion's nine betrayers**, with Selith following a planned sequence and schedule. Individual correspondences, intervals, and the result of completion remain open. Any additional sacrificed followers or bystanders are separate from those nine intended victims. Actual deaths and elapsed days depend on his means and player intervention; the plan does not guarantee its completion.
