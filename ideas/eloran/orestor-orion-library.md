# Orestor — Orion's Library (working brief)

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]] | [[Places/Orestor]] | Companion to [the opening city](orestor-opening-city.md)
> **Draft, not canon.** The library's survival, collection, staffing, and exact access rules need review. Ilrusk is publicly its librarian and volunteers in the town hospital.

Orestor claims to hold the largest surviving body of writing from Orion's own city. Other nations have copies, church selections, and fragments; this is the place where a scholar can compare originals, later copies, hostile annotations, and records the Apostolic Church would prefer to forget. Its rarity makes it a civic treasure and a religious problem. **The library is a dominant public building outside Nebeská čtvrť**; choose its district and relationship to nearby streets during the city-layout pass. The building should feel useful to ordinary people as well: clerks consult maps and contracts, physicians compare old remedies, and families seek names in plague and war rolls.

## A plausible survival

**Working history:** Orion's original collection remained in Orestor. During later campaigns against older worship and dangerous magic, destruction of the entire library would have meant burning a monument to the Church's own founder. Ilrusk helped secure a compromise: suspect works were inventoried and sealed for doctrinal review, while the public rooms remained open. He used that custody to preserve much more than the reviewers intended. How he wielded influence across centuries without exposing his identity remains open; successive librarians, hidden patrons, and his role in founding the Just are possible parts of the answer.

Ilrusk is the library's **public librarian**: he receives scholars, rules on requests, supervises copyists, and knows the collection better than anyone else in the city. He volunteers in the town hospital, especially with unusual survivor illnesses. The civic name he uses, how long he has visibly held the post, and how he conceals his much longer custodianship remain open. His private work at **Orionův starý dům** atop Nebeská čtvrť remains a separate place. A visit to the library does not grant access to the stone door or his private records.

## Rooms and access

| Area                | What is there                                                                                          | Who can use it                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Public reading hall | Civic records, maps, copied sermons, practical treatises, and a staffed request desk.                  | Petitioners, scholars, clerks, and ordinary residents during posted hours.                                                              |
| Copyists' gallery   | Collation tables, translators, repair benches, and ledgers of who requested which volume.              | Registered copyists and supervised visitors. The request ledger can become an investigative clue.                                       |
| Sealed stacks       | Conflicting accounts of Orion, pre-Church sources, early Weave experiments, and censured commentaries. | Access by written license from both civic and Church custodians, or by an exceptional court or council order. Exact rules need setting. |
| Deep store          | Originals too fragile or dangerous to handle and inventories whose omissions matter.                   | A very small custodial circle. What Ilrusk has removed, hidden, or altered remains open.                                                |

The library should answer **historical and legal questions**, not function as an oracle. A record can identify an old doctrine, a name, or a contradiction; it cannot automatically identify Bery's fragment, Selith's present host, or the powder aboard the ship. Useful research costs time, access, and interpretation. Those costs make a librarian, a copyist, a judge, or a smuggler relevant without making a single NPC hold the whole answer.

## First playable uses

- A copyist can find earlier wording for the Just's claimed right to investigate corruption, while another source disputes whether it applies inside the harbor. This can inform the firm's argument without deciding the case by itself.
- The library's access ledger could show that somebody recently sought a text on Orion's creations or the nine betrayers. Choose the reader and date only when that investigation is prepared.
- Ilrusk can meet the firm openly at the request desk or among the copyists. His interest in an unusual request should follow what he actually learns; he has no starting lead to Bery's fragment or Selith's escape.
- A Church official may demand that a controversial volume stay sealed, giving Jeroným, Benedikt, and Tillibor different reasons to care about the same institution.

## Decisions for later

Choose the library's public name, district, street approaches, other major buildings nearby, other staff and copyists, who legally owns the collection, the real access procedure, and how Ilrusk kept the archive intact. Decide how much of Orion's own work survives, whether the deep store has been altered, and what distinguishes it from the private house. Establish one specific document relevant to the ship dispute before making the library a Day 2 route.
