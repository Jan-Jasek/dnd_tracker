# The Shoalrunner Incident — three Day 1 leads

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]] | See [the opening job](orestor-opening-job.md), [city cast](orestor-opening-cast.md), and [counterfactual clock](orestor-sacred-timeline.md).
> **Working offers, not fixed outcomes.** The firm can pursue none, one, or several. The Day 3 council hearing still occurs; each completed task changes what a participant can credibly ask or promise.

The party meets three problems through the ship seizure: the watch's thin weapon intelligence, Oto's political price, and Benedikt's competing church claim. Each has an immediate scene, a reachable objective on Day 2, and a consequence beyond the ship. The bank's instruction against search and seizure remains a separate constraint.

## Rumor chain, broker, and raid — current direction

- **Lady Alynn** and other merchant republicans are accumulating weapons against a loyalist or monarchist takeover and to strengthen their bid for a capitalist republic. Bořiči know of a weapon that could turn the tide of warfare, not that the shipment is powder.
- **“Tupoun” Jožka** is the arrested chewing addict and petty thief, known to the patrol. They take him to the guardhouse to beat him; there he offers the overheard rumor to bargain for relief. Further interrogation yields no detail beyond the fragments he heard. **Jindra “Šňůra”** is the minor Bořiči messenger now in hiding; Šňůra is his nickname, and his surname remains open.
- **Lyanna, Alynn's assistant**, is Imperial, loyal to Bořiči, and attracted to Alynn. She overhears Alynn's agreement with the bank representative and later discusses the shipment with Jindra “Šňůra”. Jožka overhears that second conversation. Lyanna may withhold Alynn's dealings with the Imperial bank.
- **Captain Hans**, a western Meredian senior city-guard officer under **Sir Lothar**, receives the shipment intelligence from **Jindra “Šňůra”** and approaches **Zbožný Zdenda** to arrange the theft. Hans and Zdenda know the ship's name. Hans closely monitors investigations into Bořiči, learns in time that Jožka has named Jindra, warns him, and coordinates the raid opportunity. He intends that no guards be hurt and does not board the ship. He is a candidate for the revolutionary leader's right hand.
- **Zbožný Zdenda**, full name open, is a sincerely pious criminal patron, salvage dealer, smuggler, black-market trader, and broker. He honors agreements and supports people in his neighborhood. He suspects Hans is connected to Bořiči and wants access to the movement; Hans's exact allegiance is not confirmed to him. He funds the raid, pays the brothers, and arranges equipment and resale in exchange for a specific favor from Hans and an opening for future collaboration. The favor, loot division, and privacy of their dealings remain open.
- The raiders are three hired brothers, native to the **Skeld Isles** (working geographical name), who deserted Valdic military naval service during an earlier island conflict and have lived in Orestor for several years. Skeld lies close to the continent to the north, with a free northern part and centuries of Triskal–Valdic conflict. It is separate from the Dragon Isles far to the east. British-inspired names are a proposed cultural fit for the brothers.
- On the first night most ship crew would be asleep, with at most two people actively patrolling aboard. Shore watches are separate. The captain alone carries the cargo-hold key; the relationship between this lock, passenger baggage storage, and the concealed magazine needs a plan.
- **Mára “Bery” Beran** carries a small violet fragment wrapped in cloth in an improvised cut-out beneath a removable boot inner sole, near the arch. The boot is uncomfortable, and he favors that foot. He adjusts it privately, refuses to remove it, and is defensive about someone handling it. Ordinary baggage inspection can miss it; removal and careful inspection of the boot can find it.
- **Selith**, called **Orion** by his followers, is already active in Orestor in the existing character and Herald notes. Named early followers, his initial host or conduit, and their Day 1 whereabouts need development. His activity is independent of Bery and the fragment.

## Lyanna's source — Alynn and the bank

Lyanna overhears **Lady Alynn** speaking with the **Imperial head of Brauer & Hess's Orestor branch**. **Severin Varro** leads the local branch; his name follows the Empire's Roman-inspired naming. He is the same bank representative who approaches the firm about the detained ship. The offer combines the bank's strategic interest with Alynn's preparations to strengthen the republican cause.

**The representative's offer:**

> “Our interests align, my lady. We can put a weapon in your hands that could turn the tide of a war. Your cause would gain the means to defend itself—and a stronger position when the city's future is decided.”

Once Alynn agrees:

> “It will arrive aboard the Mělčinář. How would you like the consignment delivered?”

Lyanna learns the ship's name and the promise of a weapon. She passes that intelligence to **Jindra “Šňůra”**, who reports to **Captain Hans**; Hans approaches **Zbožný Zdenda**. All four know the name Mělčinář. Whether Lyanna hears Alynn's delivery instructions is open. She may omit the bank's involvement, protecting Alynn or concealing the source.

**Why the weapon's nature is unknown:** Alynn and the banker already have the technical description through private correspondence. Their conversation concerns political value and delivery and uses the general word “weapon”; Lyanna hears no mention of powder. The later chain passes on what she actually heard. Further investigation or questioning could reveal the powder.

**Existing delivery outline:** the captain retrieves the concealed lot during ordinary grain unloading and hands it to **Matěj Šimek**, who arranges a covered wagon and onward transfer to Alynn's reserve. The captain knows Matěj, not the final recipient. The next contact, wagon, helper, cover story, reserve custodian, destination, and proof of receipt remain open. See [the ship dossier](orestor-ship-dossier.md).

**Still to establish:** the bank meeting's time and place, Alynn's answer about delivery, what Lyanna conceals, and how the hired raiders expect to identify the weapon.

## Lyanna and Jindra “Šňůra” — the partial witness

**Established:** Lyanna is hooded when she meets Jindra. **“Tupoun” Jožka** recognizes Jindra and is intrigued by his meeting with a hooded woman whom he cannot identify. He hears only fragments about a weapon and an Imperial ship. He misses the ship's name, the bank's involvement, and the rest of their conversation. Torture cannot recover details he never heard.

**Meeting:** they meet beneath the covered riverside market in the evening before the ship's arrival, while traders pack up. The interval before arrival remains open. Jindra can plausibly be there for rope work; Lyanna conceals her face among customers in cloaks. They exchange the ship's name and practical instructions before Jožka notices them. He approaches close enough to catch “a weapon that could turn the tide” and “an Imperial ship”, but they break off when they notice him. His proximity to the stalls could also connect to his lantern-oil theft. The specific stalls, witnesses, and arrest remain open.

**Arrest and bargaining:** Jožka is arrested in the evening two days before the ship arrives, a few days after overhearing the market conversation. The patrol knows him well and takes him to the guardhouse to beat him. There he realizes he needs something to bargain with and offers the older fragments about the weapon and Imperial ship. The beating begins as routine abuse of a familiar petty thief. His offer gives the watch a potential security lead; subsequent coercive questioning cannot produce details he never heard. Hans keeps a close eye on investigations into Bořiči and learns in time that Jindra has been named. He warns Jindra before the watch reaches him. The patrol members, exact theft, officer who receives the offer, route of the report to Lothar, and means of delivering the warning remain open. Jožka has no surname in the cast.

## Bořiči leadership — current direction

The public magistrate and revolutionary **Judge** are one person, with ordinary origins and a nickname earned before judicial appointment. He appears bureaucratic, believes in democracy, and advocates destroying the entrenched order to bring **the Great Change**. The **Weaver** is a working title for a rumored hidden leader: cells may identify different inner-circle members or confuse the Weaver with the Judge. **Rewyn Ilrusk, the Weaver**, is the Judge's Weave teacher and secret patron, cultivating the movement for control of Orestor. He connects to Bořiči through the Judge alone; the Judge believes they share the purpose of his revolution. Develop the true chain of influence, the Judge's knowledge and autonomy, and who deals with lower-ranking members in [the preparation questions](orestor-preparation-questions.md).

## Jindra “Šňůra”'s investigation — to develop

Develop “Tupoun” Jožka's exact petty theft, patrol members, guardhouse location, withdrawal, testimony, and official report. He bargains at the guardhouse when the familiar patrol takes him there for a beating. The guardhouse’s duty sergeant takes Jožka’s account and reports the possible weapon shipment through ordinary watch channels toward Lothar. Hans discovers Jindra’s exposure by monitoring those reports and recognizing his name; he need not attend the interrogation. The sergeant’s identity, report wording, and distinction between the original account and later official claims remain open. Establish Jindra's shelter, protector, instructions, and several traces the firm or watch can follow. Jindra can identify Alynn's assistant, name Mělčinář, and explain his report to Hans. Lyanna heard political terms and delivery discussion; the technical description was in private correspondence between Alynn and Severin Varro. Subsequent evidence can change the revolutionaries' knowledge.

### Jindra's home, custody, and fate — open

**Current message chain:** **Lyanna → Jindra “Šňůra” → Captain Hans → Zbožný Zdenda**. Jindra delivers the message directly to Hans. He can identify Lyanna and Hans, knows the ship's name and weapon rumor, and has no view of the movement's full leadership. Whether he knows Hans's dealings with Zdenda requires discussion.

**Home:** Jindra is an Orestor local with family history in the city. He owns a small house in the crowded suburbs near the port. Exact location, present occupants, and what the watch or players could find inside remain open.

**Neighbors' observations:** Jindra has frequent visitors, often hooded. Neighbors saw guard-like armor, including chain mail, beneath one visitor's hooded outer clothing; they believe he was probably a guard, since armored visits are unusual there. They also believe some visitors were women based on body size. These are witness interpretations, not confirmed identities. They remember Jindra leaving that particular night with a large man whose steel armor made considerable noise. They cannot identify Hans by name; they assume only that the armored man is a guard or soldier.

**Chronology to settle before clues:** the riverside conversation precedes Jožka's arrest and bargaining by a few days. Jožka's arrest is in the evening two days before the ship arrives. Hans learns of Jindra's exposure and takes him to the safehouse that night. The watch begins looking for an Imperial ship before it arrives. Day 1 is Jindra’s second day in hiding, and he is growing impatient. The exact date of the earlier riverside conversation remains open. Discuss one timing decision at a time before fixing the pickup, witnesses, or trail.

**Day 1 safehouse sequence:** on the night following Jožka's evening arrest, Hans arrives with an urgent warning along the lines of “Get up. The guards are coming for you.” Jindra obeys before he has time to consider it, and Hans takes him to a Bořiči safehouse. Hans leaves a Bořiči member he trusts to watch Jindra while he attends to other business; this watcher is separate from the warehouse custodian. The watcher must prevent Jindra leaving, using force if necessary. Hans is containing an exposed messenger who can identify him and Lyanna; Jindra is unable to leave freely. Jindra begins doubting Hans's intentions and considers slipping away to a friend's home. The refuge is an upstairs room over a port warehouse accessible through Lyanna’s merchant-guild connections. Hans has no prior arrangement for sheltering Jindra there: he goes to find Lyanna himself urgently that night, keeping Jindra with him or immediately nearby because the city watch is looking for him. Hans does not find Lyanna at her house. He brings Jindra to the Merchants’ Guild and asks a doorman for her. She has a room there where she sleeps when work runs late. Lyanna accompanies Hans and Jindra from the guild to the warehouse and arranges access at short notice, dealing personally with the reluctant custodian. The warehouse custodian, **Vít Sýkora — warehouse keeper**, is common-born and dependent on his guild employment. He sympathizes with Bořiči but is too afraid to become a member. He reluctantly admits them through his trust in Lyanna, then finds himself unable to get rid of Jindra and his watcher. He does not know how to undo the arrangement safely. Lyanna’s exact address, the warehouse, custodian’s identity, watcher’s identity, rules for visitors and escalation, and longer-term arrangements remain open; Jindra is at the safehouse in the Day 1 baseline. Hans’s urgent contact with Lyanna and the hurried arrival leave potential witnesses and traces; establish what each actually observes.

**Jindra’s watcher — identity in development:** a Bořiči member and city guard who formerly served under Captain Hans in the military. When he fled to Orestor, Hans offered him a guard job and helped him settle. He believes he owes Hans a great deal; their military relationship and Hans’s practical support explain his personal loyalty. He looted during his military service and fled when punishment became likely. **Rudolf “Rudi” Seidel** is the proposed name, with a common-born western Meredian origin. The exact military posting and looting incident remain open; their full history is not needed for his Day 1 role. He watches Jindra under Hans’s orders to prevent him leaving, using force if necessary.

**Lyanna’s lodging:** she owns a small house near the port and merchant-guild warehouses, convenient for her frequent logistics duties. Hans goes there that night with Jindra kept close but does not find her. She also has a room in the Merchants’ Guild where she sleeps when work runs late; Hans and Jindra go to the guild and ask a doorman for her. The doorman recognizes Captain Hans by name and witnesses his inquiry for Lyanna. Hans tells Jindra to wait outside out of sight; the doorman does not see Jindra. The doorman knows which warehouse Lyanna went to, but is very hesitant to identify it, show the way, or grant access: protecting guild property is part of his job. This is a matter of professional duty, not established Bořiči involvement. His own name and any further observations remain open. The house’s address and any witnesses to the unsuccessful visit remain open.

**Background to develop first:** age, family history, household, personal attachments, reason for joining Bořiči, recruitment contact, familiarity with Hans and Lyanna, and what would make him fear or trust their protection. Jindra and his friend work in a shared dockside rope workshop with a few other workers. They make, repair, and splice ropes and also work as port riggers: preparing cargo slings, fitting and operating lifting gear, securing loads, and maintaining mooring and towing lines. The workshop sends workers out to ships, warehouses, and landings for installation and repairs. The workshop’s name, ownership, and other workers remain open.

**Jindra’s friend:** a male coworker from Triskal who moved to Orestor roughly twenty years ago and grew up with Jindra. He lives a considerable distance from Jindra’s house. Étienne Durand is a name candidate. He does not know Jindra belongs to Bořiči; he has noticed Jindra acting self-important and speaking of a coming “big change.” He knows Lyanna has met Jindra a few times and assumes a romantic or sexual involvement, influenced by her reputation and Jindra’s swagger; this is his interpretation, not an established affair. They work in the same dockside rope workshop with a few other workers. His own political sympathies remain open. Jindra considers going to him if he escapes the warehouse.

**Day 1 question — Jindra’s other contacts and misleading leads:** Étienne knows Jindra met more people than Lyanna and can name some of them. Develop those people, what he actually observed, and the ordinary or unrelated reasons for their meetings. Some leads may go nowhere in this investigation; establish what can be learned or checked so that a false suspicion can be resolved. Names and connections remain open. This belongs to item 6 in the Day 1 queue.

**Václav Riedel — local armsmith, misleading contact:** Étienne has seen Jindra meeting Riedel privately but does not know their business. Jindra sells him metal fittings stolen from dockside stores: small iron or brass hardware used on boats and warehouse equipment, such as rings, hooks, hinges, and reinforcing straps. Riedel knowingly buys stolen goods for cheap materials and resale. An armsmith’s involvement may appear connected to the rumored weapon, but these transactions are an independent criminal sideline. The specific lots and meetings, and evidence that clarifies their business, remain open.

**Zbožný Zdenda — shared side work:** Étienne (Eti) and Jindra take paid rigging work for Zdenda at unofficial landings. They prepare and operate lifting gear for after-hours unloading and know the cargo is avoiding customs. Zdenda pays extra for discretion; they leave once the goods are ashore. Their work gives Étienne first-hand knowledge of Zdenda and a personal reason to avoid talking to the watch. It does not establish knowledge of Hans’s raid bargain or the Imperial shipment. Jindra’s private sales to Riedel remain separate and unknown to Étienne. The specific landing, frequency, and particular recent jobs remain open.

**Workshop and the watch:** the city watch has already questioned the workers before the players visit. The workers distrust the watch and stick together, giving little useful information about Jindra or his contacts. This solidarity does not establish shared Bořiči membership. The workshop has no established relationship with the firm, whose business generally involves offices and representatives. Its response to the firm depends on the players’ approach; concern for Jindra’s safety is a possible opening, not a fixed condition for cooperation.

**Later outcome — outside Day 1 preparation:** after a few more days, Jindra may try to escape to his friend, or Hans may arrange to kill him first. Hans considers sending him out of the city under the promise of a safe escape and having him killed on the way; this is a candidate, not a scheduled event. Timing and outcome depend on the people, circumstances, and player actions. For Day 1, establish the watcher’s visitor rules and escalation limits and what traces the firm or watch could follow. Detailed arrangements for a later escape or killing are follow-up preparation. The watcher prevents Jindra leaving, using force if necessary. His exposure threatens Hans and Lyanna, but he is also a loyal messenger and potentially useful source.

#### Investigation trail — candidates for discussion

Build the trail from the actual departure and ordinary relationships. The following are suggestions, not fixed witnesses or discoveries.

- **Pickup:** Hans takes Jindra away during the night following Jožka's evening arrest. Jindra's own house in the crowded port suburbs is the pickup candidate. Decide what he leaves behind in his hurry and whether anyone sees the actual departure.
- **Departure witness:** a neighbor sees him leave with a large, noisy, armored man, assumed to be a guard or soldier. They cannot identify Hans. Determine what they hear, whether Jindra appears willing, and what they tell the watch versus the firm. A witness may assume a guard escort is an arrest and that the authorities already know where Jindra went.
- **Ordinary home leads:** landlord, work equipment, unfinished work, or a coworker's personal belongings can identify his employer and friend. They need not reveal the safehouse address. The watch searches for a fugitive and incriminating material; the firm can learn from personal connections or the meaning of ordinary belongings. Agree on the actual items and avoid treating a missed clue as proof that all guards are incompetent.
- **Direction witness:** someone with a real vantage sees the pair go toward a particular bridge, landing, or street. Nela at the ferry steps is an existing candidate, conditional on timing and sightline. Decide what she actually saw, what she wants, and whether she reports the same observation to Fenris.
- **Work and neighborhood route:** a coworker can identify Jindra's usual associates or places he already visits. The upstairs warehouse room is reached through Lyanna’s access to guild storage; ordinary deliveries, workers, and visitors can provide leads once their actual observations are established. Establish a route to the area through these contacts that does not depend on the departure witness recognizing Hans.
- **Destination confirmation:** establish what a neighbor or delivery worker could actually observe, such as the hurried arrival, food for an unexpected guest, or someone taking over the watch. Define the evidence without making normal activity automatic proof of Bořiči membership.

**Player contacts — Session 0 follow-up:** the DM plans to ask each player for roughly two named contacts grounded in their background. Possible connections include relatives among Zbožní, friends claiming Bořiči ties, or acquaintances in a noble’s circle. These depend on player choices; no connection to the workshop or investigation is presumed.

**Remaining review within item 6:** the watcher’s proposed name and origin, and any specific witnesses or evidence needed to connect the established investigation route. His looting and flight are established; Étienne’s contacts include Lyanna, Riedel, and Zdenda. His personal loyalty comes from military service under Hans and Hans’s help finding a guard job and settling in Orestor. Vít Sýkora’s role and predicament are established. The doorman can identify the destination, but his professional reluctance must be addressed through play. Hans has ordered the watcher to prevent Jindra leaving, using force if necessary; visitor rules, escalation limits, and Jindra’s later fate remain open. Cooperation at the workshop remains responsive to the players’ approach; the guild doorman can identify Hans by name. The route is Jindra’s house → Lyanna’s empty house → Merchants’ Guild → port warehouse. Lyanna joins Hans and Jindra at the guild and accompanies them to the warehouse. The custodian is a sympathizer, too afraid to join Bořiči. Neighbors witnessed the departure from Jindra’s home but cannot identify his escort. Then choose actual people, observations, and physical clues, with more than one route to the location. The players' discovery of the safehouse and their access to Jindra are separate problems.

### Shapeshifting and interference — optional idea outside Day 1 preparation

One Bořiči member could shapeshift and actively interfere with investigations, inspired by Yoru Sulfur in _The First Law_. **Lyanna** is a possible holder of this newly developed power, with the Judge teaching her to use the Weave. Neither the ability nor that teaching relationship is established. Keep this idea outside the numbered preparation queue; it does not block Day 1. Before adopting it, discuss whether the extra complexity is useful, its place in the setting's Weave and Art rules, what can be copied, costs and limits, and clues that could expose an impersonation.

An interference attempt must follow the faction's actual information about an investigation. Define its purpose, target identity, briefing, and possible discovery; do not grant the operative knowledge of private player conversations or plans without an in-world source. Consider its effect on the watch's investigation and the no-firm timeline as well as encounters with the players.

## Zdenda's commission and the captain — in discussion

The brothers enter the captain's cabin to demand the hold key and interrogate him about weapons. The captain understands their question could expose the bank's lot and deliberately directs them to **Master Berthold Rauk's** cargo. Rauk carries mechanical inventions, particularly crossbow mechanisms, and new alloys. These give the brothers a convincing military shipment to take. Rauk also carries **Weavebreaker** among his materials; form, quantity, source, and effect remain open. Existing [Just notes](order-of-the-just-wip.md#weavebreaker-mlčoun--severite) describe it as a metal, also called Mlčoun / Severite.

The brothers kill Varek after questioning him and taking his key, eliminating a witness. He deliberately directs them to Rauk’s equipment, and the concealed bank lot remains aboard. Player intervention can change this outcome; track the sensitive lot’s actual location separately from Matěj’s belief. Portable valuables can be stolen in addition to the supposed weapon, leaving bulk grain.

**Brother names for discussion:** **Hugh Mercer — eldest brother**, **Owen Mercer — middle brother**, and **Ned Mercer — youngest brother**. These are British-inspired candidates for the three Skeld-born former Valdic military sailors. Exact roles and any nicknames remain open.

**Payment arrangement:** Zdenda funds the raid and pays the brothers himself. Hans owes him a specific favor, and the bargain opens future collaboration between Zdenda's organization, Hans's guard contacts, and Bořiči. Zdenda suspects Hans's revolutionary ties and wants access; establish whether he seeks membership, influence, or merely profitable arrangements. The intended weapon goes to Hans for Bořiči; specify ownership and division of other loot, the brothers' wages, and what counts as fulfilling the contract if the supposed weapon is the wrong cargo.

**Stolen goods: recipient and route:** **Zbožný Zdenda (Zdeněk; surname open)** receives the stolen haul from the brothers before the supposed weapon is handed onward to Hans. The brothers may first take it to their own hideout, or deliver it directly to a place Zdenda specifies; the arrangement is open. Establish the Shoalrunner’s berth, their hideout, and Zdenda’s receiving place before choosing the route and timing. A port warehouse is a candidate receiving place, not an established connection to Jindra’s refuge. Decide whether the handoff happens during the raid night or later the next day or night. The boat type, carrying capacity, ownership, landing, and onward carrying arrangements remain open. A black-painted vessel is an idea to assess against the route and normal nighttime port traffic, not a settled disguise.

**Organization-name candidate:** **Zbožní — the Pious** is Zdenda's network of salvage dealers, smugglers, receivers, and hired specialists. The name fits its patron's piety and neighborhood reputation. Membership, front businesses, and relationship with Konrad remain open.

**Location candidate:** **Černý přívoz / the Black Ferry** is a smuggling landing near Orestor. Its location, legitimate use, and relationship to Zbožní remain open; see [the city follow-ups](orestor-opening-city.md).

**Specific favor — choose one in discussion:**

- **Account book:** recover a book seized by the city watch before it reaches the magistrate. It links Zdenda's salvage business to stolen goods and exposes useful clients. He wants it intact because it also records debts owed to him. Seizure, custodian, deadline, and Hans's access remain open.
- **People who could talk:** pressure prisoners or other witnesses not to testify or disclose what they know. Persuading, bribing, threatening, or other means remain to be chosen; killing is not a required outcome. Identity, testimony, leverage, and the task offered to the firm remain open.
- **Property:** secure approval for Zdenda to buy property by bribing or threatening town officers. A riverside warehouse is a candidate because it serves both legitimate trade and smuggling. Current ownership, price, opposition, authority to approve, and existing occupants or claims need discussion.

**Hans's independent firm assignment:** Hans approaches the players on his own to discharge his obligation to Zdenda. All three favors remain open; the task involves unlawful interference, whether breaking into an evidence room, pressuring witnesses, or bribing or threatening town officers. Hans is dangerous and willing to use coercion; the firm takes difficult work and is willing to solve problems through unlawful means. Define the particular request and let the players choose their response. Hans's explanation, what he offers in return, deadline, and how much of the underlying bargain he reveals remain open. His obligation exists independently of player acceptance; define his alternative route for the no-firm timeline. See [the Day 1 favor questions](orestor-preparation-questions.md#day-1-questions--hanss-independent-commission).

**Future collaboration:** advance warning of city-watch searches is one possible continuing benefit, with terms still open. Hans's access to city-watch plans gives him no control of harbor customs or templar operations. The specific favor and future relationship are separate parts of the bargain.
