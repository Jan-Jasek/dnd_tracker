# Orestor — templar crisis and Benedikt's bargain

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]]
> Related: [[People/Benedikt]], [[Organisations/Order of the Just]], [[Organisations/Orion's Wrath]], [[Places/Ellesmere]], and [the ship opening](orestor-opening-job.md).

## Direction from the DM

Ellesmere shattered the templars' confidence. Many interpret the catastrophe as divine wrath for their abuse of power. The Veil has surged back, magical creatures appear, ordinary people awaken to gifts, and Ellesmere's survivors suffer a slowly fatal corruption. The institution's old methods cannot deal with the scale of change.

Benedikt leads the Just in Orestor and believes they must adapt. Tillibor seeks to make Hněv the sole organisation controlling magic. He may recruit magical people or enslave and use them against others. Benedikt wants an alternative to subduing the magical world by force. Local templar numbers are roughly evenly split, but Tillibor gains ground.

Templars need a means of sensing Veil; specialized seekers are a possibility. The mechanism, personnel, and accuracy remain open. Their reports now come from everywhere. Detecting Veil does not establish possession, an artifact, or an imminent threat.

## Why Benedikt can end the ship intervention

Benedikt receives more interventions than his men can meaningfully pursue. He doubts whether indiscriminate searches serve the Order's purpose. A vague detection aboard is one report among many, and he can judge that it does not justify continuing the hold. He need not believe the vessel is wholly free of magic to withdraw his claim.

This gives him discretion to bargain with the advocates. If the templars instead identify a specific dangerous artifact or witness harm, the facts of his decision change. Set what the patrol actually reports before playing the meeting.

## Favor — alternatives to discuss

- **Recover contested Just grounds or artifacts from Hněv — opening direction.** Choose the property, custodian, ownership claims, task, deadline, and consequences for Tillibor with the DM. Patrol withdrawal and civic vote require separate terms. See [the Day 1 leads](orestor-opening-side-quests.md).
- **Undermine Hněv's standing with the closed council.** Choose the support or privilege at stake. Finding real misconduct, exposing an existing secret, and fabricating an accusation are different jobs; Benedikt's willingness to use each remains undecided.

Tillibor is the established leader and available political target. Hněv has votes in the open council and influence to contest in the closed council, but no dedicated Konšel seat. Jaskell and Jimmy are named fighters, without established political offices. **Brother František** is a new candidate for Hněv's city representative: likable, politically capable, and obedient to Tillibor. His candidacy and character remain proposals.

## Higher church council — proposal

A council distributed across nations may bring together senior priests or archbishops and templar leaders within a shared church. Following Ellesmere, it must agree on a new course. Its name, membership, authority, relationship to the Apostolic and Just institutions, and succession procedure remain open.

Orestor's place as Orion's city makes representation there important. Either Jeroným or Benedikt may hold the relevant higher office; Jeroným's established title is archbishop. The choice is open, and a city council seat is distinct from membership in this church council.

Tillibor may aspire to replace that church leader and eventually control the wider order. **Brother František** could seek the vacant closed-council seat as Tillibor's local ally while Tillibor pursues ecclesiastical leadership. Jeroným remains the proposed square victim; this idea does not itself assign Benedikt a death or establish which religious office passes with the vacancy.

[[People/Selith]] is drawn to Orestor as Orion's city; route and timing remain open.

## Questions that can wait until this thread needs them

- Exact means and limits of Veil detection; whether seekers form a distinct role.
- Corruption-plague symptoms, pace, treatment, transmissibility, and consequences for named survivors such as Jeod and Kate. No immunity or exception is assumed.
- Whether Tillibor believes all his doctrine, how he secures obedience from recruited or enslaved magical people, and the terms or privileges of sanctioned gifts.
- Jimmy's hinted flame powers and their compatibility with his faction's programme.
- Internal command beneath Benedikt and Tillibor, open-council representatives, and the local Just's relationship to continental leadership.

## Superseded portrayal — preserved for reference

The earlier notes portrayed Hněv as a pragmatic fragment-hunting schism that admitted gifted fighters, while the old Just maintained indiscriminate suppression. They also assigned an internal purge to templars rejecting the abandonment of Marble Fortress procedure, and hinted that Jimmy used flame gifts against corrupted foes.

That doctrinal contrast does not govern the current portrayal. The purge's occurrence and motives, Jimmy's gifts, and any selective exceptions require fresh discussion. They must not silently establish either Hněv's tolerance or a resolved historical account of the schism.
