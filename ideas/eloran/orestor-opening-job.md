# Orestor — debt and the seized ship (WIP)

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]]
> **Draft, not canon.** Debt history follows the DM's account. The ship dispute, decrees, terms, and faction actions below are proposals for review.

## The firm's debt

The **[[Organisations/Legal Consultate|legal consultate]]** began on borrowed money. Establishing the business and maintaining its network of contacts and assets cost more than its early work brought in.

During the siege of **[[Places/Orestor|Orestor]]**, paying work collapsed. While **[[Factions/Triskal|Triskal]]** and the **[[Factions/United Empire|Empire]]** were allies, Triskal's naval blockade left little prospect of help arriving by sea. The firm's expenses continued: premises, staff, retainers, and the cost of keeping its network available. Interest accumulated even when clients could not pay.

The firm borrowed wherever it could to survive. Its obligations are scattered among private lenders, commercial houses, and personal acquaintances. War, political upheaval, and the destruction of **[[Places/Ellesmere|Ellesmere]]** killed or scattered many of those creditors. Some claims have surviving holders; others have uncertain ownership or incomplete records. The firm faces debts it cannot reliably total and demands it cannot easily verify.

**[[Organisations/Brauer & Hess]]**, already a client, offers to consolidate those obligations into one documented debt, with one creditor and an agreed repayment schedule. For the firm, this means breathing room and predictable expenses. For the bank, it secures an ongoing relationship with capable local advocates who know the city.

### Proposed opening offer

The bank acquires or settles claims it can substantiate, then agrees a consolidated balance with the firm. Unverified claims remain a named exception to resolve; a missing creditor's signature cannot simply be assumed. The paperwork, surviving claimants, and the bank's willingness to absorb disputed claims are possible future hooks.

Bank commissions pay a stated fee: part in cash to keep the firm operating, part credited against principal. Refusing one job leaves the agreed repayment obligation in place; it does not automatically trigger immediate ruin. Amount, interest, collateral, and the treatment of later claimants remain to be set with the DM.

### Player-facing brief

> “One of my employer's ships was detained by the city guard this morning. It carries a very valuable cargo. I understand there's some dispute over jurisdiction. Meanwhile, trade stands still—and my employer does not care for that. Money and goods must keep moving.
>
> “Secure the release of the ship and its cargo, and see that the consignment reaches its intended recipient. The captain knows who is to take charge of it at the dock.
>
> “And do I need to spell this out? No searches. No seizures. Do not fuck me on this.”

The representative gives no warning of theft and does not identify the recipient. The captain knows Matěj Šimek, the passenger charged with the handoff, but not the final recipient. The advocates must ensure delivery; they are not instructed to transport the goods themselves.

The representative calls it “my employer's ship” because the bank has chartered freight space and has a financial stake in the voyage. Varek operates the vessel independently and owns its grain cargo; the bank does not command his crew.

### DM scope of the original bank contract

The assignment covers release of the ship and specified consignment, followed by ensuring delivery to the intended recipient. Release from the harbor hold is an intermediate objective. If the Bořiči steal the cargo, locating and recovering it remains part of the same assignment; there is no new hiring. This contingency belongs in DM preparation, not in the opening speech.

The bank presents the shipment as commercial business and explicitly forbids search and seizure. The working hidden cargo is new southern Imperial powder, supplied through Jeod's connections to strengthen the republicans. Orestor has no express ban on its possession or sale; concealment from harbor customs creates the immediate legal and safety dispute. Define the consignment, ownership, intended use, applicable declaration rules, and proof of completed delivery. Varek knows the lot is undeclared and that Matěj is to take it from his custody, but does not know where Matěj will ultimately deliver it. Matěj's knowledge of the contents and hiding place is open; the proposed first-night false alarm works if he does not know the hiding place.

The amethyst is a passenger's personal possession, outside this contract. **Jeod learns of its arrival later** and then issues the separate amethyst commission through the bank.

### Cargo, allies, and recipients — working direction

**Alynn knowingly participates** to strengthen the merchant republicans. Imperial assistance serves the Empire by weakening Triskal's claim on Orestor; the Empire need not acquire the city or attach political obligations to make that worthwhile. Mandatory Imperial protection or covert annexation is not part of this proposal.

The merchant republicans and socialist Bořiči share opponents but have conflicting long-term economic aims they have not yet fully recognized. The Bořiči do **not know the cargo's contents**. Their unnamed leader may be close to Alynn and suspect that something important is aboard; that relationship and suspicion remain proposals, not knowledge of the gunpowder.

Before play, define the declaration and safety duties that apply to the concealed powder, its intended local recipient and use, and how it will be handed off. Alynn's participation does not establish that the entire guild, Konrad, or crew knows. Decide each participant's knowledge separately. The working [ship dossier](orestor-ship-dossier.md) gives Varek his own grain cargo, a separate concealed dry compartment for the powder, and named crew and passengers, including Matěj; the final beneficiary remains open. Bery's personal stone is independent of this shipment.

### Ship and passenger design — next discussion

The working vessel is a familiar, long-serving coastal and delta trader. It survived the Shattering, then profited by carrying scarce goods among major ports. The proposed [ship dossier](orestor-ship-dossier.md) develops its sail, sweeps, shore towing, grain hold, concealed magazine, named crew and passengers, separated cargo and baggage, and possible search outcomes. Master Rauk, an independent dwarven weaponeer, travels with apprentices; one has stolen jewelry and a local craftsman may frame their master. A commercial agent is to receive the undeclared lot. Lukáš Dohnal, a Meredian deserter, hides from recognition; infamous bounty hunter Hynek Vrána travels without suspicious freight. The Novotní have several children and are hiding what they did to afford passage. Mára “Bery” Beran carries the amethyst personally.

## Council powers — proposed model

The **Veřejná rada (open council)** makes the city's standing laws, including commercial rights and the jurisdictions of its institutions. The **Konšelé (closed council)** govern and execute those laws, with powers to issue administrative decrees and emergency declarations. Closed-council members also influence open-council votes through their guilds, clients, and political followings.

An executive decree can order action within those powers. Whether an emergency declaration permits an exception to a standing law is the contested boundary. Sponsors within the closed council claim that the ship requires immediate action; their opponents argue that the conditions for using emergency powers have not been met. Who reviews that claim, and how quickly, remain prep decisions.

The **city guard** is Lothar's police force. The **harbor guard** is Konrad's customs and port service: it administers admission, manifests, duties, cargo inspections, and harbor enforcement. The two have separate commands. These roles and the legislative/executive division are draft setting proposals.

## Four instruments behind the dispute

Working wording for player-facing extracts; titles and exact legal limits remain subject to review.

1. **Closed council — decree on Imperial commerce.** “Imperial-owned cargo is subject to an extraordinary enemy-trade levy. Goods and their carrying vessel may be held pending declaration of ownership and security for payment.” This penalizes commerce without automatically making every passenger a criminal. The levy, scope of Imperial ownership, and treatment of existing contracts need setting.
2. **Open council — harbor jurisdiction law.** “Within the harbor precinct, vessel admission, customs examination, and cargo custody belong to the harbor service. The city guard may not assume these functions under its ordinary authority.” This identifies who performs port duties; it does not authorize every possible search.
3. **Closed council — emergency substitution decree.** “Where an immediate threat to the city requires action, the city guard may temporarily assume the duties of another civic service to contain that threat.” The guard must identify the claimed threat and record its assumption of authority. Who can invoke this power and how it is reviewed remain open; the declaration is the document the party can challenge.
4. **Open council — guarantee of free commerce and lawful possession.** “Peaceful commerce and lawful possession stand under the city's protection. Beyond ordinary customs formalities, compulsory search or seizure requires stated grounds relating to an offense or immediate danger. Travelers may not be compelled to submit to questioning or detention solely by reason of foreign origin.” A levy on goods does not itself establish grounds for a security search or detention of their carriers.

Routine customs checks of manifests, declared goods, and duties remain possible. Opening sealed consignments for a security investigation is the disputed additional intrusion. The free-trade guarantee gives merchants grounds to resist arbitrary interference; it does not exempt them from all customs duties or investigation.

## The ship dispute — proposed situation

An Imperial-linked vessel receives harbor admission. The new commercial decree passed during its voyage; delayed notice to the harbor is an available complication. The exact Imperial connection could be registration, ownership, or ownership of particular cargo and needs choosing.

The **city guard identifies the vessel as an enemy-linked threat** after interrogating a captured Bořiči runner. Under torture he repeated a rumor that a weapon aboard an arriving Imperial ship could be used to change Orestor's government. He had not seen the weapon or the ship; he heard it from another member, who had heard it from someone preparing to steal the freight. The guard matches the rumor to the arriving vessel, invokes emergency substitution, and places a cordon at the landward exit of its berth. It demands custody of the vessel, access to cargo, and compulsory questioning of passengers. The rumor is incomplete and coerced, yet the working hidden gunpowder means the threat cannot simply be dismissed as invented.

The **harbor guard retains the berth and vessel access** under its standing jurisdiction. It refuses to surrender the cargo or admit a city-guard search party. If an examination is justified, customs officers will conduct it. It also disputes whether the emergency decree has been properly invoked.

The **Merchants' Guild represents affected owners and travelers**. Its members refuse consent to an extraordinary search; its advocates challenge both services under the free-trade guarantee. It demands concrete grounds for compulsory passenger questioning or detention and for opening sealed goods. The guild's practical resistance consists of owners withholding access, masters retaining keys and seals, and representatives filing a formal protest. It does not command either force; overriding that resistance would require coercion under contested authority. Some local craft masters nevertheless want the visiting dwarven weaponeer **Master Rauk** kept out of Orestor. One working [ship-dossier subplot](orestor-ship-dossier.md) has armsmith Václav Riedel pay a porter to plant a forged Imperial commission in Rauk's tool chest, then demand a search and detention. The attempt is interruptible. This protectionism conflicts with the guild's broader free-trade case; it does not give the craftsmen power to arrest him.

Passengers are **held in practice** by the cordon while the guild challenges the legality of their confinement. They have not all been arrested or individually accused. Harbor admission, restraint of the vessel, cargo inspection, and personal detention are separate questions, which the guard's emergency claim attempts to combine.

### The three parties at the quay

| Party                                   | Demand                                                             | Refuses                                                                            | Possible concession                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **City guard / [[People/Lothar]]**      | Emergency control, cargo search, and passenger questioning.        | Unloading or release while it claims a threat remains.                             | Specific interviews by agreement, customs-led examination of identified goods, or release against a recorded guarantee. |
| **Harbor guard / [[People/Konrad]]**    | Recognition of its exclusive port and customs duties.              | Transfer of custody or a city-guard search under a disputed emergency declaration. | A narrowly scoped customs inspection with agreed witnesses, if grounds or owner consent permit it.                      |
| **Merchants' Guild / [[People/Alynn]]** | Release of unaccused passengers and respect for lawful possession. | Compulsory questioning or extraordinary cargo searches without specific grounds.   | Voluntary interviews and a limited inspection protecting seals, records, and owners' claims.                            |

The city guard claims **necessity**; the harbor claims **jurisdiction**; the guild demands **lawful grounds**. Customs authority answers who may inspect, but leaves the scope and justification of this particular search in dispute. Each party risks conceding a precedent that will weaken it in the next case.

**Council politics:** Lothar's loyalists can justify restrictions through Jan's protectorate agreement, which includes clearing Imperial influence. **[[People/Oto|Oto]]** can support penalties on an enemy's commerce while rejecting Triskal's authority. Alynn defends the open council's trade protections. Sponsors, votes, and concessions remain open. Lothar commands the city guard; Oto's armed household is a separate force.

The cordon is a temporary coercive fact, not a settled legal outcome. The guard can try to board, customs can obstruct it, and merchants can refuse access; none wants to take responsibility for the resulting clash. A review deadline, expiring emergency order, or negotiated settlement can break the impasse. Choose the review authority and timing before play so an appeal has a concrete destination.

### The emergency — proposed political provocation

A faction within the closed council wants a precedent for overriding the republicans, Merchants' Guild, and harbor service, whose interests converge around free trade. **Alynn and Konrad themselves sit on the closed council**; this is a struggle among its members and their supporters. The sponsor and participating councilors remain open.

**Working trigger:** a captured Bořiči runner gives the guard the weapon rumor under torture. The runner is two retellings away from the would-be thief and knows neither the weapon's contents nor the intended recipient. The guard can claim an imminent Imperial-backed attempt to change the government without having evidence that any passenger is an Imperial soldier. The report may conceal how the information was obtained or exaggerate the runner's firsthand knowledge. The closed-council sponsor sees a chance to expand the guard's emergency powers; the rumor can be exploited politically even if the sponsor did not invent it.

Distinguish the torturer and recorder of the statement, the official who endorses the emergency, and the officers who enforce it. Lothar's involvement is open: he might see the raw statement, receive only a cleaned-up intelligence report, or know how much uncertainty has been removed. Ordinary guards need not know the source or who benefits.

Give the claim evidence the advocates can examine: the runner's detention record, a polished guard report that calls hearsay “intelligence,” a possible witness to the interrogation, and the chain of Bořiči members who repeated the rumor. A suspiciously prepared demand for lasting city-guard control over port security can reveal political exploitation. Following the chain may lead toward the attempted theft. Finding gunpowder validates one part of the rumor, but does not prove the runner knew its nature or that the guard lawfully seized everyone aboard.

The prize is institutional power. A search that finds nothing need not make the sponsors withdraw: they can claim suspects escaped because customs obstructed them. The party can challenge the guard's account of the tortured runner, negotiate limits on the emergency, or force a public review. Exposing how the report was obtained and exaggerated changes political leverage even if some form of cargo settlement is still required.

## Pressure and player choices

Suggested deadline: the next sailing tide. Missing it increases costs and the captain's desperation. Decide the actual interval before assigning timeline dates.

Possible approaches include a joint inventory, release of uncontested goods into a guarded warehouse, an appeal over the law's scope or effective date, or a negotiated bond. No single solution is required. A guild guarantee or a harbor favor creates a relationship that can matter later. A paid smuggling offer can expose the party to **[[Organisations/Mlcenlivy|Mlčenliví]]** without revealing Konrad's hidden identity.

**Bořiči first-night theft — working version:** if the cargo dispute remains unresolved on the first night after the firm is hired, the Bořiči strike absent effective intervention. The harbor permits Varek to remain aboard as custodian while most crew and passengers are held in a nearby building; the guard watches the gangway and landward quay. The Bořiči arrive by water, abduct Varek, and take portable declared goods. They do not know where the hidden powder is, so it remains in the concealed compartment unless they later force Varek to reveal it or another event changes custody. Because the captain and visible goods are missing, Matěj believes the sensitive lot was taken too and reports a loss he cannot verify. Their leader may suspect its importance through a possible relationship with Alynn, without knowing its contents. They take Varek to question him about the rumored weapon, since they cannot find it by searching blind. Set their route, the goods taken, where they hold him, and how long he can keep the compartment secret. Telegraph preparations so player intervention can change the outcome.

The abduction sharpens the jurisdiction fight. The city guard calls it evidence that the harbor failed to secure an enemy-linked vessel and demands control of the ship. Harbor customs points to the guard's landward cordon and insists it still has authority to inspect cargo. The guild argues that confining innocent travelers did nothing to protect their property. The party can pursue Varek, challenge the reported loss, seek a limited search, or negotiate access to the hold; the powder's location should be discoverable through play, not protected by the mistaken report.

The **man held by the watch** is the captured Bořiči runner. He was detained before the ship arrived and gave the coerced account that triggered the seizure. He knows the member who told him the rumor, but not the source's actual identity or where the hidden freight is kept. Following that chain is one route to the would-be thieves, within the existing bank assignment; no one hires the firm again if cargo is stolen. A search of the ship, a recovered-cargo inventory, or Varek's testimony can reveal that the powder was never taken. A successful theft gives the authorities someone to blame and endangers the intended delivery even while the kegs remain aboard.

**Religious intervention — branch to choose:** templars report a sensed Veil presence aboard and invoke an ancient doctrine granting a church militia authority to investigate magical threats. They are following a magical trace, **not the guard's weapon rumor**; their interest might turn toward passengers rather than freight. With the Veil surging after Ellesmere and many refugees visibly sick, their report cannot by itself distinguish Bery, Růžena, an artifact, or ordinary background corruption. The doctrine's wording, scope, standing under civic law, and means of detection remain open. The Just and Hněv each have roughly half the local templars and both claim protective duties.

- **Benedikt route — preferred idea for discussion:** the intervening templars belong to the Just. The advocates are sent to Benedikt, who asks a favor in exchange for withdrawing his interest in the cargo or accepting its release. He is overwhelmed by similar reports and questions the policy of pursuing every magical trace in a world full of returning Veil. A vague detection need not justify continued detention in his judgment. The favor would anger Tillibor: recovering disputed relics from Hněv or undermining its support in the closed council are the current options. Actual discovery of a dangerous artifact would change the grounds for his decision. [Schism and bargain](orestor-templar-schism.md).
- **Hněv route — alternative:** Tillibor's templars intervene directly. The advocates' interference with their holy mission creates the conflict. This does not also assume a separate Just intervention.

Suspicion aboard, detection of a magical presence, identification of the stone, and physical custody are different states. Decide the selected branch's detection limits and information before scheduling action; neither branch automatically finds Bery or the crystal.

**Desired consequence for later jobs:** the firm's work on the ship case brings it into conflict with the Just / Hněv. Develop a concrete demand that conflicts with its client's interests, the protection of passengers, or the negotiated settlement. Actual hostility should follow what the firm does and what Hněv learns. This gives Tillibor leverage and a reason to remember the advocates when he later commissions the killing investigation. The disputed demand and his means of pressure are the next opening-job decisions; the disagreement need not reveal the amethyst.

## Bery and the amethyst

**Working proposal:** Bery is one of the held passengers and carries the amethyst on his person. It is separate from the commercial cargo and from the bank's contract. Physical custody and information leaks are tracked from arrival in [the sacred timeline](orestor-sacred-timeline.md). Jeod learns of the stone's arrival later.

**Introduction in play:** the party sees Bery repeatedly argue for permission to leave. Each time he approaches the cordon, the guard rejects him and sends him back to the other passengers. He is helpful and easy to like, then grows wary when anyone looks near his coat. He wants to reach family in Orestor and quietly look for a buyer for the amethyst. He does not reveal that purpose merely by asking for release.

The [ship dossier](orestor-ship-dossier.md) develops Bery as a sick Meredic cart driver who acquired the stone only a few weeks ago. Its previous custodian and the circumstances of that transfer remain open. He intends to stay with family, with a sister Eva Beranová as a working contact, then seek a buyer. The [older Milbern–Iskelny material](orestor-fragment-plot.md) is a parked idea and todo, not a source of assumed facts for this opening.

The Bořiči theft targets portable cargo and Varek; the hidden powder stays aboard in the working version. They can seek it later if Varek reveals the compartment or they gain new information. Moving the stone requires a specific event involving Bery: a personal search, handover, theft, loss, or departure. Release lets him leave with it unless events establish otherwise. Neither a cargo inventory nor resolution of the bank's case automatically discovers or transfers it.

**Searches are possible outcomes.** Plan a cargo search and a personal search separately, including what each can find and who receives seized property. Finding the crystal can begin a pursuit of its custodian, a custody dispute, or an early recovery offer. Keeping it from discovery until a scripted date is not required.

**Corruption — proposal to develop:** the crystal provokes possessiveness and conflict. A person who takes it might disappear with it under suspicious circumstances. Set onset, resistance, symptoms, the person's motive and opportunity to leave, and evidence of their route before using this outcome. Disappearance is not automatic: containment, vigilance, or player intervention can prevent it. Preserve actual custody even if it shortens the planned recovery job. Detection limits and search scope can give the opening room to develop without guaranteeing the stone remains hidden.

**Council hearing — likely route:** a closed-council session could resolve the competing civic claims and introduce Oto and the monarchists. Set how the advocates obtain a hearing, when it occurs relative to the first-night theft, what the council can decide, and whether its ruling binds the religious claim. The earlier noble / purkrabí job remains undeveloped; a hearing need not require completing that job first.

## Independent follow-up commissions

An incident still to be chosen prompts a hunt for the amethyst. Once Jeod learns of it, the bank offers **cancellation of the consolidated debt** for recovery in a **Severite / Mlčoun-lined box**. Its assignment concerns only the stone; the bank has no knowledge connecting it to the ritual attacks. Dialogue, hunter interests, and containment questions: [the amethyst commission](orestor-amethyst-commission.md).

Separately, the Heralds' rites establish their presence and punish perceived oath-breakers without requiring the stone. **Tillibor may approach the firm to investigate Jeroným's death**, following the proposed square incident. His reason for employing outsiders and the job's terms remain open. The order of the two offers depends on their own triggers: [Herald rites and investigation](orestor-herald-rituals.md).

The bank's proposed lead is a description of the passenger from the opening, seen with the crystal. Make Bery recognizable during the detention scene; the sighting and report to the bank happen through means still to be chosen. The carrier or a useful lead may leave the city, while Tillibor wants the culprit found before another killing. Selith's working plan is nine deaths echoing the nine betrayers, on a schedule still to be developed.

A further candidate job concerns influencing an **open-council vote for a closed-council seat**, potentially Jeroným's vacancy. The patron, candidate, procedure, requested intervention, and deadline remain open. These overlapping obligations make time and relationships matter. Refusal is possible but difficult for the firm to afford; define the bank's financial terms and Tillibor's actual leverage.

## Risks or contradictions to resolve

- Physical arrival and discovery are separate events. Decide when the party identifies the stone; meeting Bery does not reveal his possession. Jeod's discovery follows arrival and is independent of the cargo contract.
- Templar involvement in the hunt requires an information trigger. Sensing corruption aboard does not automatically locate or identify the stone; the branch and detection limits remain open.
- Bery's acquisition and future route need detail. The parked imported plot supplies no default patron, companions, appraisal visit, or escape sequence.
