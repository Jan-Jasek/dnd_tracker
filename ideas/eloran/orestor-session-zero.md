# Orestor — the firm's prequel (session 0 draft)

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]]
> **Draft, not canon.** This is a guided way for the players to establish the [[Organisations/Legal Consultate|firm]] together. Their answers become the firm's actual history; the suggested cases and lines below can change at the table.

Use short narrated openings, one concrete choice in each scene, and a cut forward after the consequence. The GM controls the historical events; the players decide what their characters did, who they trusted, and what the firm became. No scene should require a particular PC to be a lawyer, founder, hero, or debtor. Resolve professional work through testimony, negotiation, favors, and risk as well as rolls. The session ends before the live seized-ship assignment.

## Before play

Tell the players that the group is a small Orestor advocate firm that survived the Third Meredian War and owes scattered debts. Ask for each character's role in its work, including investigators, guards, fixers, clerks, and advocates. Let each player choose **when their character joined**: before Ellesmere, amid the catastrophe or siege, or after the coup. In earlier scenes they can briefly play a client, colleague, or witness, or simply help frame the firm's past. Share the broad public sequence: Ellesmere fell in the Shattering; an Imperial column besieged Orestor; Triskal turned on that column; the purkrabí's protectorate agreement, coup, and compact left the city contested. Keep the fragment, hidden powder, Jeod's ownership of the bank, and Selith out of this briefing.

Keep a visible **firm ledger**. Each player contributes one useful asset or contact, one person the firm owes, and one person who would still come to them for help. Let the group choose its name, premises, and a principle it claims to defend. If they cannot settle a detail immediately, leave a blank and return to it after a scene.

## Scene 1 — Before Ellesmere: an ordinary case

_“Before the catastrophe, the sign on your door still looked new. A client has brought you a dispute the established houses would not take. The fee will barely cover the work. Across the desk sits someone who needs an answer before the next market day.”_

Choose a modest, local case with the players: a seized cart, an unpaid crew, a threatened eviction, or another problem fitting their characters. Ask each participating PC what they do that the firm could not do without them. Let the group choose a method: make a legal claim, bargain with someone powerful, gather awkward evidence, or solve the client's immediate need first. Give their choice a result and a cost. It should establish **how the firm works**, not test whether it deserved to exist.

Record the client's name or role, an ally earned, and someone who resents the firm's method. Ask two players whose characters are present how they first came to trust, depend on, or doubt each other during this case. Make another bond when a later character joins. A bond can be professional or personal and need not be friendly.

## Scene 2 — The night Ellesmere falls

_“The city receives contradictory reports from the capital. Roads fill with people who left before the messengers did. The earth has moved; then come accounts of impossible creatures and new magic. No one at your desk yet knows what truly happened at Ellesmere.”_

Present two simultaneous claims on the firm's limited time: a frightened newcomer needs shelter or representation, while an existing client needs a document, witness, or escort before officials close a route. Ask whom the firm helps first, how it handles the other request, and which PC sees something that changes their view of the Just, the city authorities, or magic. Let both people remain in the city as future contacts or critics. The players can hear rumors about the disaster, but do not give them hidden cosmological truth.

Record one person saved or failed, one rumor the firm believed, and one asset, contact, or assumption damaged by the aftermath. The Shattering precedes the Imperial siege of Orestor in the current [city history](../../data/Eloran/Places/Orestor.md); keep that order even if the exact gap remains open.

## Scene 3 — The siege: keeping the doors open

_“The Imperial column is outside Orestor. Trade has narrowed to a trickle; a blockade has made help by sea uncertain. Clients need advocates, but few can pay. Rent, wages, retainers, and favors still come due. Someone at the firm has found a lender willing to advance enough to keep the doors open.”_

Put a concrete petition before the players: a grain seller wants permission to move food, detained workers want release, or a family needs a passage claim honored. Have an official, a merchant, and an ordinary claimant ask for incompatible concessions. Let the party choose the case's resolution and what it is willing to pledge for survival. Borrowing is part of the firm's shared history; the lender, collateral, and personal cost are theirs to define. It can be several small loans, a favor guaranteed by a PC, or an obligation inherited from an earlier business expense.

Record a surviving creditor or claimant, a debt whose holder is now uncertain, and one choice made under siege that a current faction could remember. Do not make a single failed roll close the firm. The dramatic consequence is what they owe or whom they disappointed. The siege's precise relation to Triskal's break with the Empire needs a dated chronology before the narration states who controls the blockade at a specific moment.

## Scene 4 — After the coup: the firm in the new city

_“The Imperial army is gone. The purkrabí's protectorate agreement has reached the city, and the men who deposed him do not agree on what Orestor should become. Your old creditors are dead, displaced, or suddenly represented by strangers. The firm still has its sign, its people, and a ledger nobody can fully reconcile.”_

Ask which relationship the firm preserved through the coup, which institution now distrusts it, and what promise it made to keep operating. Put the chosen contacts into the new city's merchant, guard, harbor, temple, or neighborhood networks only as the players' answers justify. If they want to take a clear political side, let them; otherwise the firm can have clients across the divide.

Close with a short montage of the following year: one case paid in kind, one demand for repayment, one colleague or client who vanished, and the first work the firm did for [[Organisations/Brauer & Hess]]. The bank is already an established client by the campaign opening. Its consolidation offer can now be attractive because the players know exactly what scattered claims cost them. End at **PS 2 M02**, as a representative requests a meeting about a detained trader. Let the first full session begin with that offer and the live [ship dispute](orestor-opening-job.md).

## Carry forward into play

Before ending, read the ledger back to the group and confirm it. Put the chosen people and obligations into the opening cast or contact list; do not make every connection a trap. A useful record is:

| From session 0      | Write down                                                  | First-session use                                                |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The firm's identity | Name, premises, roles, public principle                     | How clients address them and what conduct would cost reputation. |
| People              | Ally, critic, client, surviving creditor, missing claimant  | A witness, recommendation, demand, or competing account.         |
| Debt                | Why they borrowed, from whom, pledged asset, disputed claim | Concrete terms to negotiate with the bank.                       |
| Political memory    | Siege decision, coup relationship, view of magic or church  | Which official starts with trust or suspicion.                   |
| Party dynamic       | Two or more bonds made through actual choices               | Who speaks for the firm when interests diverge.                  |

Do not assign automatic mechanical rewards or penalties before knowing the player characters and rules at the table. Contacts, access, and obligations are enough to make these choices matter. After session 0, update the [[Organisations/Legal Consultate|firm's page]] and the campaign state with the players' answers.

## Prep still needed

- Fix the siege's exact interval and the blockade's commander against the PS 0 chronology before writing a date into the read-aloud text.
- Pick or co-create the modest case and siege petition around the characters; choose a few recognizable officials or witnesses without making the prologue an exposition tour.
- Decide how much debt the bank can substantiate and what a remaining disputed claim could cost; put the exact figures in the player's consolidation documents.
- After character creation, trim scene prompts that duplicate a PC backstory and use that backstory as the source of a contact or obligation.
