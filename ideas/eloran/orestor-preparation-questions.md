# Orestor — preparation questions in discussion order

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]] | See [the opening job](orestor-opening-job.md), [the Day 1 leads](orestor-opening-side-quests.md), [city contacts](orestor-opening-city.md), and [the ship dossier](orestor-ship-dossier.md).
> **Discussion queue.** Develop one topic at a time with the DM. Names, motives, locations, and outcomes listed as questions are not established answers. List each character with their known title, nickname, and alias; distinguish those from discarded working names.

## Details established for preparation

- **Lady Alynn** and other merchant republicans are accumulating weapons against a loyalist or monarchist takeover and to strengthen their bid for a capitalist republic. Bořiči know of a weapon that could turn the tide of warfare, not that the shipment is powder.
- **“Tupoun” Jožka** is the arrested chewing addict and petty thief, known to the patrol. They take him to the guardhouse for a beating, where he offers the overheard rumor to bargain for relief. Further interrogation yields no more detail. He needs no surname. **Jindra “Šňůra”** is the minor Bořiči messenger now in hiding; Šňůra is his nickname, and his surname remains open.
- **Lyanna, Lady Alynn's assistant**, surname open, is Imperial, loyal to Bořiči, and attracted to Alynn. She overhears Alynn agreeing to collaboration with the bank representative who later approaches the firm: a weapon that could turn the tide of a war will arrive aboard Mělčinář. The representative asks Alynn how she wants it delivered. Lyanna is the other speaker whom Jožka overheard with Jindra; she may conceal Alynn's dealings with the Imperial bank when passing on the intelligence.
- **Captain Hans**, Meredian from the west, is a senior city-guard officer under **Sir Lothar**. Jindra “Šňůra” reports the shipment to him; he approaches Zbožný Zdenda, monitors investigations into Bořiči closely enough to discover Jindra's exposure in time, warns him, and coordinates the raid opportunity. He intends that no guards be hurt and does not board the ship. He is a candidate for the revolutionary leader's right hand.
- **Zbožný Zdenda**, full name open, is a sincerely pious criminal patron, salvage dealer, smuggler, black-market trader, and broker. He honors agreements, supports people in his neighborhood, and suspects Hans's ties to Bořiči. He funds the raid and pays the brothers for a specific favor from Hans and an opening for future collaboration. Lyanna, Jindra, Hans, and Zdenda know the ship's name; Jožka hears only part of the conversation and learns no name. Alynn and Severin Varro know the technical description through private correspondence; Lyanna hears only the political offer and delivery discussion.
- The raiders are three hired brothers, native to the **Skeld Isles** (working geographical name), who deserted Valdic military naval service during an earlier island conflict and have lived in Orestor for several years. Skeld lies close to the continent to the north, with a free northern part and centuries of Triskal–Valdic conflict. It is separate from the Dragon Isles far to the east. British-inspired names are a proposed cultural fit for the brothers.
- On the first night most ship crew would be asleep, with at most two people actively patrolling aboard. Shore watches are separate. The captain alone carries the cargo-hold key; the relationship between this lock, passenger baggage storage, and the concealed magazine needs a plan.
- **Mára “Bery” Beran** carries a small violet fragment wrapped in cloth in an improvised cut-out beneath a removable boot inner sole, near the arch. The boot is uncomfortable, and he favors that foot. He adjusts it privately, refuses to remove it, and is defensive about someone handling it. Ordinary baggage inspection can miss it; removal and careful inspection of the boot can find it.
- **Selith**, called **Orion** by his followers, is already active in Orestor in the existing character and Herald notes. Named early followers, his initial host or conduit, and their Day 1 whereabouts need development. His activity is independent of Bery and the fragment.

## The Judge and the rumored hidden leader

**Current direction:** one person is both the public magistrate and the revolutionary **Judge**. The nickname predates his judicial appointment. A miller's son placed in charge of the workers, he became their dispute-settler and carried that role elsewhere. He has calm natural authority; the city says he can tell guilt by looking at someone, a rumor whose supernatural basis is not established. He rose from ordinary origins and appears to be a conventional bureaucrat. He believes in democracy and considers destructive revolution necessary to remove the entrenched rulers and introduce **the Great Change**, a working slogan. His conviction that power will not be surrendered voluntarily is part of his political outlook; his methods and consequences require discussion.

**The Weaver — Ilrusk; title subject to naming discussion.** Lower-ranking Bořiči may disagree about who actually leads them, whether the Judge and Weaver are one person, and whether the title refers to one or several people. Different members may identify different figures from the inner circle as the Weaver. Establish which of the six local organizers handle ordinary recruits, messages, and operations, and what each cell believes.

**Ilrusk — established hidden patron.** **Rewyn Ilrusk, the Weaver**, cultivates Bořiči as one of several means to retain control of Orestor. **The Judge** is his Veil disciple and secret intermediary and believes they share the purpose of his revolution. Ilrusk connects to the movement through the Judge alone. Develop the Judge's knowledge of his teacher's ancient identity, his autonomy, and what each other leader knows; distinguish this actual relationship from ordinary members' rumors. See [[People/Ilrusk]] and [[People/The Judge]].

The DM cites _The Wisdom of Crowds_ as inspiration. The Great Change does not establish a successful revolution or a fixed outcome.

## Day 1 questions — inner-circle knowledge and arrangements

These questions belong to items 1–2 in the numbered Day 1 working scope. Unanswered details remain open across incremental commits.

- **Knowledge:** what does each member know about the Judge, the Weaver, the other organizers, the bank, and the shipment? What do they deliberately conceal, and which figures do ordinary members believe to be the Weaver?
- **Recruitment and contacts:** who recruits whom, who meets ordinary members, and how do messages reach the leadership? Establish Jindra “Šňůra”'s immediate contact and instructions.
- **Meetings:** where and how are gatherings arranged and protected? The nobleman and bishop are the visible meeting figures; decide who chairs, who recruits, and how they divide duties.
- **Relationships:** develop individual loyalties, rivalries, dependencies, and disagreements, including Hans's relationship to the Judge and Lyanna's attachment to Alynn.
- **Limits:** what will each organizer refuse, tolerate, conceal, or authorize in pursuit of the Great Change? What would make them defect, expose another member, or seize control?

## Day 1 questions — Hans's independent commission

This belongs to item 7 in the numbered queue and remains part of the Day 1 working draft. Hans approaches the players on his own; the favor is his obligation to Zdenda.

- **Choose the request:** account book from the evidence room, pressure on prisoners or other witnesses to stay silent, or bribery or threats to secure a property purchase. All three remain open.
- **Define the target:** person, evidence, or property; what Zdenda needs changed; who stands in the way; what makes the interference unlawful.
- **Define the offer:** why Hans chooses the firm, what he pays or promises, what he reveals about Zdenda, and how much of the raid bargain he conceals.
- **Set the timing:** when on Day 1 Hans approaches, the deadline, and the task's relationship to the ship dispute and other work.
- **Set the consequences:** who notices the interference, who benefits or suffers, and what evidence of Hans or Zdenda's involvement could remain.
- **Keep the baseline independent:** what Hans does if the firm does not exist or declines, and how Zdenda responds to failure.

Hans and the firm are willing to use coercion and unlawful methods to solve clients' problems. The particular actions and consequences depend on the task and player choices.
