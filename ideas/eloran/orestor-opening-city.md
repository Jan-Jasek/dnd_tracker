# Orestor — city state at the Shoalrunner's arrival

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]]
> **Draft, not canon.** This is the street-level companion to [the opening cast](orestor-opening-cast.md) and [three-day counterfactual](orestor-sacred-timeline.md). New names, establishments, and local practices are candidates for discussion. The firm's regular haunt should acquire its real history from the players.

He knows older prayers and can challenge the idea that every tradition before Orion was evil.       | He is neither a confirmed prophet nor an automatic sanctuary from templars. His account of Orion is testimony and tradition, not established cosmology. |

**Einar's legend, as he tells it:** his god foresaw a child of a demigod and a demon named Orion, who would become greatest among the gods and end the war among demons, gods, and humankind. The god willingly let Orion consume his essence. Einar says this is why the shrine survived the Apostolic purge of older worship. Whether any part of this happened, and why the shrine really survived, are open. The existing account of [[People/Orion]] calls Orion mortal; preserve that contradiction as an in-world claim. The god's name is still open; candidates for a Nordic-sounding invented name are **Árvend**, **Veyr**, and **Aldvar**.

The people above are not universal informants. Each learns from a specific vantage: Hana from records, Nela from the ferry steps, Kuba from the water, Jitka from her pupils, and Einar from visitors and inherited stories. Giving them different fragments of information makes the city investigable without a single all-knowing contact.

## Underground and civic follow-ups

**Bořiči leadership — current direction.** The public magistrate and revolutionary **Judge** are one common-born figure, nicknamed before appointment, who appears bureaucratic and seeks democracy through the destructive **Great Change**. Cells may disagree about a hidden **Weaver** and identify different inner-circle members as that leader. **Rewyn Ilrusk, the Weaver**, is his Veil teacher and hidden patron, reached only through the Judge. The Judge believes they share the purpose of his revolution. Develop six local prominent members, with Ilrusk as a hidden seventh, their birth status, actual relationships, and contacts with ordinary recruits in [the preparation questions](orestor-preparation-questions.md).

**Zbožný Zdenda — independent criminal patron.** A sincerely pious salvage dealer, smuggler, black-market trader, and broker who supports his neighborhood and honors agreements. He suspects Hans is connected to Bořiči and seeks access to the movement. He funds the raid, pays three professional Skeld brothers, and arranges equipment and resale in exchange for a specific favor and an opening for future collaboration. Captain Hans approaches him after Jindra “Šňůra” reports the shipment; both know the target ship is Mělčinář. Full name, premises, exact favor, loot division, privacy of their dealings, and ambitions toward Bořiči remain open. Zbožní is the proposed name for his organization. Choose Hans's specific favor from a seized account book, prisoners or witnesses, or civic approval for a property purchase; Hans approaches the players independently on Day 1 to carry it out; the task involves unlawful interference and all three options remain open. Kuba Sedlák remains a candidate waterside contact with no fixed role in the weapon rumor or raid.

**Černý přívoz / the Black Ferry — location candidate.** A smuggling landing near Orestor. Exact location, access, ordinary ferry or salvage activity, control, and ties to Zbožní remain open.

**Mlčenliví — proposed revision to existing canon.** The [current organisation file](../../data/Eloran/Organisations/Mlcenlivy.md) describes an old, ranked thieves' and assassins' guild. A leaner version would make the Silents a handful of unnatural killers controlled by Konrad for Ilrusk, not the city's general criminal marketplace. Ordinary theft and smuggling would belong to groups such as Zbožní, while Konrad keeps the underground on a short leash through port access, favors, and selective enforcement. Fenris could remain his harbor informant and the party's indirect contact with Konrad; meeting Fenris need not mean meeting a killer. The number, nature, and limits of the killers, and which old named members survive the revision, are open. Until that choice is promoted into canon, the two versions must not be treated as simultaneously true.

**Václav Riedel** remains a local armsmith and guild member worried that Master Berthold Rauk will win repair contracts. He can complain or compete in public; he has no forged-commission scheme or paid porter.

**Judge — follow-up.** Give Orestor a magistrate whom the firm can petition when the closed council cannot agree on which decree governs the harbor. Set appointment, independence, and the scope of interim relief; the earliest ordinary audience would be Day 3 unless the party finds a lawful emergency route. A judicial ruling on the civic decrees need not decide the Just's church claim.

**Street cast — follow-up.** Develop Zbožní's street contacts and an opposing crew, the Split Oar and Three Currents innkeepers, a library copyist, and a judge's clerk only when those contacts can appear. Anna Kovářová already runs the Copper Seal. Their daily wants and one useful lead matter more than full biographies.

**[Orion's Library](orestor-orion-library.md) and city layout — focused follow-up.** The library is a dominant building outside Nebeská čtvrť; its precise district belongs to a later pass on city layout and major buildings. The institution and Ilrusk's public role have a [working brief](orestor-orion-library.md). Its survival, public rooms, restricted records, and legal access merit fuller preparation before it becomes a research route in play.

## Taverns and inns — candidate venues

| Venue                                   | District and everyday clientele                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Use in play                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Copper Seal** (_U Měděné pečeti_) | A modest tavern near the council clerks' route, frequented by petitioners, messengers, and tradespeople waiting for a decision. **Working regular place for the firm.** Proprietor **Anna Kovářová** keeps a back table where they can meet clients. | A familiar social base: debts are overheard, witnesses can find the advocates, and Anna can say who came asking for them. Let session 0 establish why the players trust her, what they owe her, and whether they rename the place. |
| **The Split Oar** (_U Zlomeného vesla_) | A rough quay tavern for tow crews, sailors, skiffmen, and night-shift customs workers.                                                                                                                                                               | Tracks a missing captain, stolen goods, waterside routes, and who is paying for silence. Kuba and Fenris's people may both be seen there, for different reasons.                                                                   |
| **The Three Currents** (_Tři proudy_)   | A cheap Nové město inn for displaced families, provincial traders, and people waiting for onward boats.                                                                                                                                              | A plausible room for the Novotní or other released passengers, and a place where church charity, Herald recruitment, and ordinary need collide. Rooms and beds are scarce after Ellesmere.                                         |

No venue protects a fugitive merely by being a tavern or shrine. The proprietors have to decide whether to admit, refuse, report, or hide someone, and those decisions should follow what they know and owe.

## Still to settle before running these scenes

- Name the old god, decide who allows the shrine to function, and what relic or seasonal rite is visible there.
- Place Eva Beranová's home and set whether she knows the shrine keeper or any smuggler.
- Set the firm's history with its regular tavern in session 0; the Copper Seal is a usable default.
- Give Jitka's students identities only when the players can meet or protect them. Decide whether Hněv has already heard of her lessons.
- Fix the quay sightlines, the skiff's landing, and the distance from customs to the passenger building before staging the raid or passenger release.
