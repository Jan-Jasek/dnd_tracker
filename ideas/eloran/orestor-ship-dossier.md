# Orestor — detained trader and passenger dossier

> Part of: [[Campaigns/Orestor]]
> **Ship design draft.** Use with [the bank's opening job](orestor-opening-job.md) and [the custody timeline](orestor-sacred-timeline.md). Names, numbers, cargo lots, and individual histories are proposals until discussed with the DM.

## Vessel and history

**Working type:** a shallow-draft coastal and lower-river trader. Sail is its main propulsion along the coast and with favorable winds. Two long sweeps help with turning, berthing, and gentler reaches. Against a strong current, the crew poles in shallows or hires a shore team and towline where the bank allows it. Neither method makes a laden ship fast upriver; depth, wind, current, and labor set the pace. It has no paddlewheel.

The captain and this ship have worked the delta ports for roughly two decades. The Shattering's earthquake and tsunami destroyed many vessels on exposed eastern coasts, including several in his small trading concern. This ship survived PS 0 in a sheltered side channel farther upriver. It now circles the main Red River delta ports, including Orestor and Triskal's east-delta port, and makes opportunistic trips upriver. A working route for this voyage is **Imperial island-fort → Meredian landing on the safer western side of the river → Orestor**, skirting Triskal-held waters and taking on passengers in Meredie. The particular Meredian stop, previous sea leg, next port, and sailing deadline remain open.

**Candidate ship:** _The Shoalrunner_, locally **_Mělčinář_**, a familiar sight at the three old trade-coalition ports: Orestor, the Triskal east-delta city, and the Imperial island-fort. _Second Tide_ / _Druhý příliv_ is an alternative if the crew renamed it after surviving the disaster. **Candidate captain:** Varek, an independent and sharp trader from the Empire's southern maritime frontier facing Vilmar across the sea. He smokes cigars made of rolled tobacco leaves or another setting-appropriate herb, but forbids flames below deck. He knows the bank's lot is undeclared and knows which passenger will take it from him, but **does not know the final recipient**. He does not personally move the hidden lot or supervise every passenger's baggage. His surname, home port, and ownership arrangement remain open.

The Empire has Roman/Byzantine roots and many subject cultures. A Hungarian-inspired community on its southern maritime frontier is a possible fit, not established canon. **Váradi Bálint** (family name first in that culture; **Bálint Váradi** in a foreign manifest) is a candidate full name for the captain; **Varek** can remain his current working name until the DM chooses. This needs a named province or port before promotion to canon.

**Working scale for play:** ten crew including Varek, eleven proposed individual passengers including a bounty hunter, and the Novotný family. Jakub, Alena, Běla, and Ondřej are named so far; they may have more children, so the passenger total is open. A short enclosed passenger space and a covered deck area make for a crowded voyage; the family is not secretly involved in the freight. The bank has chartered space for its lot, which lets its representative call this “my employer's ship” without making Varek a bank employee or the bank the hull's owner. Final tonnage, berth plan, and which crew members know about the concealed lot remain open.

The bank orders the advocates to release the ship and cargo without searches or seizure and to ensure delivery to its original beneficiary. Varek can identify Matěj as the authorized handoff, but cannot name that beneficiary. Matěj controls the next link; whether he knows the powder's nature, its quantity, its hiding place, or the recipient remains open. In the first-night theft version below, **Matěj does not know the hiding place**; Varek can verify the kegs without a structural search. The bank representative does not tell the party. Alynn knowingly participates in the Imperial aid plan; what she knows of individual containers and agents remains to be set.

**Powder in this setting — working proposal:** southern Imperial powder-making is a recent craft. Small charges can serve mining, demolition, or crude siege weapons; neither common firearms nor a settled Orestor trade regime follows from its existence. Possession and sale are not expressly forbidden. Concealing a hazardous imported lot and omitting it from the manifest would still create a serious customs and safety dispute if discovered. Whether any particular duty, declaration rule, or wartime decree applies remains to be written. The city's rumor calls it a regime-changing weapon; that is a political interpretation, not proof of what Alynn intends to do with it.

**Hold layout:** the main hatch opens to a central cargo space stacked with Varek's grain sacks. Cloth, iron fittings, and medicines occupy clearly marked smaller lots; crew and passengers keep personal chests outside the freight space. Behind a removable section of the dry side lining, above the damp bilge and away from the galley and lamps, sits a compact concealed magazine for the sealed powder kegs. Grain hides the access panel from a glance, but powder is not mixed into the food or packed in ordinary grain sacks. The captain controls the hatch and compartment; who else aboard knows how to open it remains open. This design makes the concealed lot difficult to find during a manifest check, while a close structural search can find it.

**Intended transfer:** normal unloading begins with the grain. At an agreed point, Varek would bring the concealed lot out and hand it to Matěj, who arranges a covered wagon and onward delivery. Matěj need not know where it was stored before the handoff; the captain knows no final recipient. The exact wagon, helper, cover story, and what Matěj has been told remain open. If the cordon prevents unloading, the kegs remain aboard.

**First-night theft — working version:** if the dispute lasts into the night and the party does not prevent it, most passengers and crew are kept in a harbor building while Varek is allowed to remain aboard. The guard watches the gangway and landward quay. Bořiči reach the vessel by water, overpower and abduct Varek, and take portable declared goods such as cloth, medicines, or fittings. They do not know of the concealed compartment, so the powder stays aboard. The deserted deck, opened hatch, missing goods, and missing captain make **Matěj and the others believe the sensitive consignment was stolen too**. This is a mistaken report, not a transfer of powder custody. They take Varek to question him about the rumored weapon, because searching the hold blind has failed; set their escape route, his place of captivity, and how long he can resist. If he reveals the compartment under pressure, or the party searches the ship, the situation changes. The crime and false assumption should leave fair evidence: a selective theft of portable goods, an untouched part of the hold, and no recovered powder among the stolen lots.

The city guard's secondhand weapon rumor, the actual powder, and the templars' magical trace are separate facts. The guard obtained the rumor by torturing a Bořiči runner, then connected it to this Imperial ship. The templars have a different reason to investigate. The real cargo can partly vindicate the rumor without making every guard claim true or every detained passenger suspect.

## Mára “Bery” Beran — stone carrier

**Origin and former life:** Mára is Meredic, born in a village on the road to Ellesmere. He drove a small goods cart into the capital and was at its edge when the Shattering struck. He survived, spent the following days hauling injured people toward improvised shelters, and now shares the wasting illness visible in many survivors. Friends call him **Bery**.

**How the stone reached him:** he acquired the wrapped amethyst only **a few weeks before the opening**, while moving among the post-Ellesmere refugee routes. Its previous holder, the exchange, and any earlier custody are open. He recognizes its value but knows nothing of Orion, Erathon, Selith, or Jeod. The working custody chain begins at the unresolved acquisition, then runs **Bery → Varek's ship**; pin down the transfer before play. No part of the parked Iskelny material supplies a default answer.

**Why he is here:** he means to stay with family in Orestor, then quietly find someone willing to buy the stone. A sister, **Eva Beranová**, is a working family contact; her home, livelihood, and willingness to shelter him remain open. He has no arranged buyer or fixed handoff. Treatment and cash also matter. Weeks of carrying the amethyst have made him watchful and possessive; he worries that officials or fellow travelers have guessed what he has. How much of that is the stone's influence remains open.

**What the players can meet:** a gaunt cart driver who still carries a small brass bell from his harness, remembers people's names, jokes with the children, and helps where he can. Then he checks the packet under his coat and reads a harmless glance as suspicion. He repeatedly asks to leave the cordon, but can still be warm and persuasive when he asks the legal firm for help. If freed, his first intended stop is his family's home; the address and his next move should be chosen before play. A later sighting of him with the stone can reach the bank through an independent route, but this profile does not fix the incident that starts the hunt.

## Rauk's rival — attempted frame

**Working antagonist:** Václav Riedel, a Meredic armsmith with German-influenced family roots and a workshop in Orestor. He fears that a Lapiberg master will win scarce repair contracts and undermine the local guild's prices. He wants Rauk held through the next sailing tide, not necessarily convicted. His allies can demand a licensing check in public while he privately pays quay porter **Pavel Žák** to fabricate something sharper.

**Plan:** once the guard's weapon rumor spreads, Riedel prepares a forged Imperial commission claiming that Rauk agreed at the island-fort to supply spring-locks for soldiers. Pavel is to slip the paper into Rauk's tool chest when passenger baggage is moved or examined, then claim to have seen him receive an advance. Riedel will offer the guard this “lead” and press for a personal search. The accusation exploits the guard's existing fear but has no connection to Matěj's actual hidden gunpowder. Rauk has not agreed to such a commission. If the party protects access to the chest or intercepts Pavel, the false paper never appears there; the complaint can still be challenged as unsupported.

**Discoverable faults:** Riedel's complaint can be dated before the alleged evidence was found; the captain's port log may contradict Pavel's account of when Rauk went ashore; the chest seal or knot may have been disturbed; Pavel may be seen entering under a porter pass or paid from Riedel's workshop. No single clue must be mandatory. If a broad search also finds Sivra's genuinely stolen bracelet, that gives a separate accusation against her, not proof of Riedel's charge. Alynn's guild has to decide whether to defend free trade consistently when one of its own craftsmen wants the guard to seize a rival's tools.

## Search and movement

Map the gangway, passenger space, cargo hold, hatch and seals, waterside access, and route from berth to land. Record who has keys and which areas city guard, harbor customs, merchants, templars, crew, and passengers can reach under each claimed authority. The first-night Bořiči theft targets portable freight and Varek; their waterside access and escape route must be possible from the actual quay layout. Bery's stone changes hands only after a personal search, voluntary handover, theft of his effects, or another recorded event.

If a search finds the hidden powder, the city's political evidence changes, though possession alone proves no statutory ban was broken. If it finds the amethyst, track the actual finder and custodian and use the early-discovery branch. Finding one does not automatically reveal the other. If Matěj escapes with the lot, follow his handoff; if detained, the chain stalls. If the vessel sails, record the captain's next port and whether passengers remain aboard.

## Česky — podklady pro hru

**Loď:** _The Shoalrunner_, v Orestoru zvaný **_Mělčinář_**. S Varkem brázdí říční deltu zhruba dvacet let. Jde o obchodní loď s malým ponorem pro pobřeží a dolní tok Rudé řeky. Na moři a po proudu používá plachtu. Dlouhá vesla pomáhají při manévrování; proti silnějšímu proudu posádka používá bidla nebo si najímá lidi či tažná zvířata na břehu a loď táhne lanem. Plně naložená loď tak proti proudu nepluje rychle. Při katastrofě přečkala ve chráněném říčním rameni výše proti proudu. Kapitán přišel o několik jiných lodí, které zastihla vlna na otevřeném východním pobřeží. Pro tuto cestu se počítá se zastávkami u císařské ostrovní pevnosti a v Meredii před Orestorem; loď se vyhýbá triskalským vodám. Na palubě je deset členů posádky, jedenáct navržených jednotlivých pasažérů a rodina Novotných. Zatím jsou pojmenovaní rodiče a dvě děti; dalších dětí může být víc, takže celkový počet pasažérů zůstává otevřený.

**Kapitán Varek:** samostatný, ostrý obchodník z jižního pobřeží Císařství, naproti Vilmaru přes moře. Kouří doutníky ze svinutých listů, ale pod palubou zakazuje otevřený oheň. Do Orestoru veze vlastní zásobu obilí; ve městě je po válce a přerušení lodní dopravy vzácné a dobře placené. Zná Matěje Šimka jako pasažéra, který má převzít nepřiznanou zásilku, ale **nezná konečného příjemce**. Matěj má zařídit další předání. Jestli ví, co přesně přebírá a kde je zásilka ukrytá, zůstává otevřené; ve verzi noční krádeže skrýš nezná. Varek není zaměstnavatelem zbrojíře ani účastníkem krádeže jeho učednice. Pro kapitána je ve hře i jméno **Váradi Bálint**; zda zůstane Varek, ještě není rozhodnuto.

**Náklad a podpalubí:** Banka si na lodi pronajala místo pro svou zásilku; Varek podniká samostatně. Veřejný soupis uvádí jeho obilí a zásilky sukna, kování a léčiv od různých vlastníků. Hlavní poklop vede do prostoru s pytli obilí a označeným zbožím. Za odnímatelnou částí bočního obložení, nad vlhkou stokou a dál od kuchyně i lamp, je malá suchá skrýš se zapečetěnými soudky nového prachu z jihu Císařství. Obilí kryje přístup, ale prach není nasypaný do pytlů s jídlem. V Orestoru není jeho držení ani prodej výslovně zakázán; problémem by bylo zatajení nebezpečného nákladu před celníky. Přesné znění předpisů, množství, vlastník a plánované použití zůstávají otevřené. Beryho ametyst je jeho osobní věc, nikoli součást nákladu banky.

**Vykládka:** Nejdřív by šlo z lodi běžné obilí. Ve smluvenou chvíli by Varek vyzvedl skrytou zásilku a předal ji Matějovi, který zajistí krytý vůz a další předání. Matěj nemusí vědět, kde byla ukrytá. Varek konečného příjemce nezná. Pokud hlídka vykládku zastaví, soudky zůstanou na lodi.

**Noční krádež — pracovní verze:** Zůstane-li loď zadržená, většina posádky a pasažérů stráví noc v přístavní budově; Varek smí zůstat na palubě. Stráž hlídá lávku a přístup ze břehu. Bořiči připlouvají od vody, přemohou a unesou kapitána a odvezou snadno přenosné přiznané zboží, třeba sukno, léčiva nebo kování. O tajné skrýši nevědí, takže prach zůstane na lodi. Zmizelý kapitán a náklad ale přimějí Matěje i ostatní domnívat se, že zmizela také bankovní zásilka. Jde o mylný závěr, nikoli o skutečnou změnu držitele prachu. Varka unesou, aby jim řekl, kde je údajná zbraň; je třeba určit únikovou cestu, místo výslechu, jak dlouho může vzdorovat a stopy, podle nichž lze omyl odhalit.

**Posádka (10):** Varek velí; Irena Sol vede záznamy o nákladu; Boran Kett je lodník a sjednává tahouny; Dalia Fen vaří a hlídá zázemí. Olek přišel o rybářskou loď, Petra Voss zná přístav, Radan zná říční mělčiny, Sorel je nový plavčík, Tavin přežil katastrofu v říčním rameni a Ysra nastoupila až v posledním přístavu. Kromě Vareka nikdo z nich nemusí znát pravý účel bankovní zásilky.
