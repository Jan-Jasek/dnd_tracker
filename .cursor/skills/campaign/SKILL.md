# Campaign

Use this skill to turn world canon into playable campaign structure and to keep campaign state current as players act.

## Workflow

1. Read all relevant canon files first — campaign, location, faction, and recent timeline.
2. **Guide one decision at a time.** Check what the DM already knows but hasn't written about the current decision. During collaborative preparation, follow the guided discussion below.
3. Check existing world canon before inventing any new campaign facts.
4. Separate campaign-facing truth from hidden world truth.
5. Convert player actions into timeline events, changed NPC positions, faction pressure, rumors, and future hooks. Write the **current** campaign situation; do not keep a history of superseded prep or DM decision traces (`no longer`, `rather than` the previous plan).
6. Keep session prep practical: scenes, stakes, likely NPCs, clues, encounters, choices, and consequences.
7. Use templates in `templates/` when creating new campaign files.
8. Leave generated prep as `draft` or `candidate` until the DM promotes it.

## Guided Preparation

- Ask **one substantive question per turn**, selected because its answer constrains the next preparation step. Explain briefly why it comes next; avoid lists of loosely related questions or a single question containing several decisions.
- Offer a short recommendation or two or three meaningful options if useful, then wait for the DM's answer before drafting dependent material.
- Record agreed answers, distinguish tentative proposals, and continue to the next decision. Keep unanswered dependencies and optional ideas in a parked queue; show a full recap only when requested.
- Establish what happens, when, who is involved, and what each actor knows before designing the investigation clues. Distinguish when information was heard, reported, and acted upon; these need not occur on the same day.
- Build the sequence around the DM's current topic and prior answers. Do not jump from one unfinished topic to several new side plots, NPCs, or locations.
- Honor requests for a complete draft, broad brainstorm, or recap. Execute direct edits and already-authorized work without unnecessary questions.

## Campaign State Bias

Track:

- Active threads
- Open questions
- Player decisions
- Consequences in motion
- Faction clocks
- NPC goals and reactions
- Location changes
- Rumors entering circulation
- Next-session hooks

## City Sandbox Guidance

For faction-heavy city campaigns:

- Give each faction an agenda, resources, weakness, public face, and hidden truth.
- Use clocks for escalating plans.
- Let player actions change faction priorities.
- Surface hidden changes through rumors, altered locations, missing NPCs, market shifts, laws, violence, ceremonies, and social pressure.
- Propose faction turns, but do not treat them as canon until reviewed.

## Session Prep Output

Useful session prep usually includes:

- Recap
- Starting situation
- Likely scenes
- Active NPCs
- Active factions
- Secrets and clues
- Encounters
- Rumors
- Rewards
- Consequences to track after play
